## TL;DR

1. **`form` is a metadata type.** A `form` item is `name` + `object` + the form body. Its name is `OBJECT.KEY`, the name the form is served under today, so no served name changes ([D2](#d2), [D3](#d3)).
2. **It passes ADR-0088's admission test on three consumers that exist today**: the anonymous form doors, an action of `type: 'form'`, and Setup's public-forms list. A record create or edit form is not `form` content ([D1](#d1)).
3. **Adding the type is additive, with one write narrowed.** The release that adds the type is major 19 (`7142585849`, rulings 1 and 2). The dual-read exists on `main` between the cards' merges and in no release: there the loader projects the three old shapes into `form` items, `view` reads are unchanged, and nothing an author wrote stops loading. After the major the projection serves no content, and only the kept reader and rule 11's check remain ([D7](#d7), "After the major"). One package-less save is refused, and some forms of it are admitted today (items 9 and 10, [D5](#d5)).
4. **The anonymous doors and the withdrawal judgement read both types through one projection.** A form switched off in either type is closed in both; a stored row that closes an address by replacing its base's form keeps replacing it when the base moves to `form`; a stored body the projection cannot read closes the slugs it names ([D6](#d6)).
5. **The old shapes are tombstoned only in the next protocol major**, with an ADR-0087 conversion for sources and a ceremony for stored rows, `.custom` overrides included. A stored row that keeps a public address closed, by its switch or by replacing its base's form, is moved, or by the later ruling left as stored, and is never deleted or offered for deletion ([D7](#d7)).

## Context

### What `view` carries today

One type carries two unrelated things. `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts` declares:

- the form body, `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts#FormViewSchema` (`type`: `simple`, `tabbed`, `wizard`; `sections`; `sharing`; `submitBehavior`);
- the container, `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts#ViewSchema`, with four members: `list`, `form`, `listViews`, `formViews`;
- the ADR-0017 item, `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts#ViewItemSchema`: `{ name, object, viewKind: 'list' | 'form', config }`, one arm per kind, and its wire twin `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts#ViewItemWireSchema`.

So a form reaches the platform in **three shapes**: the container's `form`, a `formViews` entry, and a `viewKind: 'form'` item. `form` is not a member of `packages/spec/src/kernel/metadata-plugin.zod.ts#MetadataTypeSchema`.

### No record form reads a form view

Since #36425 the console's create dialog, full-page create, action-opened create and edit lay out by the object's `fieldGroups` and the rules on its fields. The docblock of `ui/packages/app-shell/src/utils/recordFormNavigation.ts#FormViewDefinition` says it in these words: "No record create or edit reads one". `addRecord.formView` is a tombstone in `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts`. The lint rule `form-view-internal-not-loaded` (`packages/lint/src/validate-internal-form-views.ts`) reports an internal create or edit form as steering nothing. No per-role or per-position form assignment exists.

### Named forms have three consumers

1. **Public forms.** `GET` and `POST` under `/api/v1/forms/:slug` (`packages/rest/src/form-routes.ts#registerFormEndpoints`), rendered by the console at `/f/:slug`.
2. **A form an action opens.** `packages/spec/src/ui/action.zod.ts#ActionType` has the member `form`; its `target` is the form's name, routed to `/forms/:name`, where `ui/apps/console/src/components/FormPage.tsx` reads `GET /api/v1/meta/view/:name`.
3. **Administration and design surfaces**: Setup → Configuration → Public forms (#36697, merged as `c45dd80ff9`), the Studio Developer → Public Forms page, and previews.

Nothing on the list side reads a form: the view switcher takes list items only (`ui/packages/app-shell/src/providers/MetadataProvider.tsx` routes `viewKind: 'form'` items away from `listViews`), and an app navigation entry's `viewName` targets a list (`packages/spec/src/ui/app.zod.ts`).

### The anonymous doors and the withdrawal judgement

Both doors read the form through one resolution inside `packages/rest/src/form-routes.ts#registerFormEndpoints`: it lists type `view` (the effective read, beside the per-package layer), and offers a form only when `packages/spec/src/ui/anonymous-form-intake.ts#anonymousFormIntakeCandidates` yields it. That function scans the three shapes in a fixed order. A form is open when its `sharing` declares `enabled: true`, `allowAnonymous: true` and a `publicLink`; ADR-0138 D2 row 1 names exactly this as the doors' admission condition.

`packages/spec/src/ui/anonymous-form-intake.ts#anonymousFormIntakeWithdrawnIn` is the kill switch: a body of the same name that keeps the link and sets a switch to `false` closes the form, matched by slot or by slug, and layering may only narrow. Identity is the item **name**. The object a form submits into is resolved by `packages/spec/src/ui/anonymous-form-intake.ts#anonymousFormObjectName` over four rungs, one of which is the container's `list.data.object` — a form reading its binding off a list.

Two write-time judgements read the same candidates: the tenancy intake advisory in `packages/metadata-protocol/src/runtime-authoring-gate.ts` (it returns nothing for a type other than `view`), and the container name-collision refusal in `packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-save.ts`.

**No door refuses a second form on a slug another form already publishes.** The resolver takes the first candidate in list order; the header of `packages/spec/src/ui/anonymous-form-intake.ts` says "Another row that publishes or withdraws the same slug is a different form and closes nothing". The only clash refusal is client-side, in the paused designer.

### Loading, overriding, translating

- The loader expands a container into items: `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts#expandViewContainer`, called from `packages/metadata/src/plugin.ts`, `packages/objectql/src/engine-registration.ts` and, for stored rows, `packages/metadata/src/view-container-expansion.ts#expandRuntimeViewContainer`.
- `view` is `listed` in `packages/spec/src/kernel/customization-package.ts#OVERRIDE_TYPE_CLASSIFICATION`. Setup's switch saves a `view` override in the app's paired `.custom` (`ui/packages/app-shell/src/views/setup/publicForms.ts#switchPublicForm`), a body that holds the switch and nothing else of its own.
- Form copy is translated at `objects.OBJECT._views.KEY.title` and `.submitBehavior.title` / `.message`, and section copy at `objects.OBJECT._sections.SECTION` (`packages/spec/src/system/translation.zod.ts#ObjectTranslationDataSchema`; emitted by `packages/lint/src/i18n-walk.ts`).
- The stored-row conversion pass wraps one row in **its own type's** stack collection and reads that collection back (`packages/spec/src/conversions/stored.ts#applyConversionsToStoredItem`). It cannot move a row from one type to another.

## Decision

### D1 — `form` is admitted as a metadata kind, on consumers that exist {#d1}

ADR-0088's three clauses, each on a fact measured above:

1. **Independent lifecycle.** A form is created, published, switched off, overridden and deleted under its own name. The public address and the action target each name one form. Nothing reads it as a facet of a list: the switcher and the app navigation take lists only, and a form shares with its object's lists only the `OBJECT.` prefix.
2. **Declarative governability.** It ships as a standalone document from a package, is authored at runtime through Studio and the metadata API (`allowRuntimeCreate`), is versioned, and takes a declared override ([D4](#d4)).
3. **A real consumer.** The anonymous form doors serve it; an action of `type: 'form'` opens it; Setup switches it. All three exist before this record.

**What is `form` content, and what is not.** A `form` item is a named form that something opens: a public form (it declares a `sharing.publicLink`), or a form an action of `type: 'form'` targets. A "custom create form" or a "wizard" is `form` content only when an action opens it. The object's record create and edit form is **not** a `form` item (ruling item 3): it is the object's `fieldGroups`, and the type has no `isDefault`, no `create` or `edit` role and no key that binds a form to a record page, a role or a position. A request for a per-role record form is a new decision, not a reading of this one.

**A `form` item with no consumer is reported, not refused.** `os validate` and the lint report an item that declares no `sharing.publicLink` and that no `type: 'form'` action in the stack targets, as a warning — the successor of `form-view-internal-not-loaded`, by the same matching rule (the target must equal the name the form is served under). It is a warning because a form is legitimately consumer-less for a moment while it is being built: the form is saved first and the action that opens it second. An item that declares `overrides` is judged with its base: it is not reported when the base's form of that name has a consumer. An override with no link over a linked form has a job, which is to keep that address closed ([D7](#d7)), and a warning that it steers nothing would invite its removal.

### D2 — The shape: `name`, `object`, and the form body at the top level {#d2}

```ts
// forms: [ … ] in a stack; one document per form
{
  name: 'crm_lead.web_to_lead',   // OBJECT.KEY — D3
  object: 'crm_lead',             // the one binding
  label: 'Web to lead',           // optional; the row's name in lists
  type: 'simple',
  title: 'Contact us',
  sections: [ /* … */ ],
  sharing: { enabled: true, allowAnonymous: true, publicLink: '/forms/contact-us' },
  submitBehavior: { kind: 'thank-you', title: 'Thank you' },
}
```

- **One Zod source** (Prime Directive 7): a `FormSchema` in `packages/spec/src/ui/`, derived **by reference** from `FormViewSchema`'s shape, never transcribed. `FormViewSchema` stays as the body schema; the schema-bound Studio forms and the React form block keep using it.
- **The body is at the top level.** There is no `viewKind` and no `config` wrapper: the type is the discriminator, and "body = today's form view schema" is read literally. Ruled so ([question 5](#q5), which keeps the wrapped alternative as the option not taken).
- **Identity keys**: `name` and `object` required; `label` optional (the display name in lists and in Setup; when absent a surface shows `title`, then the name). The item carries the envelope every standalone item carries: `protection`, the ADR-0010 fields, and ADR-0146's `overrides` declaration.
- **Not carried over from the view item**: `viewKind`, `config`, `isDefault` (a record-form role, ruling item 3), `order` (the switcher's order) and `scope` (the registry's provenance already says package or runtime).
- **The object binding has one rung.** A `form` item's object is its `object`. The body's `data` key is not authored on a `form` item: the schema refuses it by name with the prescription "bind the form with `object`". A form bound to a JSON Schema (`data.provider: 'schema'`) is not a `form` item — it has no consumer among the three. The four-rung resolution of `anonymousFormObjectName` survives only inside the projection of the old shapes ([D5](#d5)) and leaves with them.
- **`object` must name an object** the package can reference, judged where a view's object is judged today (`os validate`, publish). It is not editable after the form is created: the name carries it ([D3](#d3)).
- **Stack collection** `forms`; file patterns `**/*.form.ts`, `**/*.form.yml`, `**/*.form.json`; items are served at `/api/v1/meta/form`, as every type's are.
- **The `*.form.ts` suffix goes to the new type, and the Studio modules take `*.metadata-form.ts`, in card ②.** Today the suffix means a Studio metadata-form module: 17 files carry it, all under `packages/spec/src`, and `scripts/i18n-bundle-surface.mjs#isMetadataFormModulePath` classifies by it on the file's base name anywhere in the repository. Left as it is, an example's `lead.form.ts` would be read as a Studio module by the two readers of that predicate (the gate derivation in `scripts/pm/dispatch-gates.mjs` and the landing reading in `scripts/pm/landing-k.mjs`). So the 17 modules are renamed to `*.metadata-form.ts` — the name of their helper (`defineMetadataForm`) and of their translation family (`metadataForms`) — and `scripts/i18n-bundle-surface.mjs#METADATA_FORM_MODULE_SUFFIX` becomes `.metadata-form.ts`.
  - **This follows question 1 by the seat's routing, not by the ruling's words.** Ruling record 7131569242 settles the factory name. It does not mention the suffix. The owning seat routed the suffix to follow the name by the same logic (the obvious spelling goes to the new type, the Studio one moves), in seat record [`7132202077`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/pull/36957#issuecomment-7132202077) on PR #36957. It is written here so the approver reads it as the seat's routing and can strike it; the alternative not taken is to keep the 17 file names and narrow the predicate to `packages/spec`.
  - **Who reads the suffix, measured at `689e5695b98`** (card ② counts again at its own head):
    - the one matcher, `scripts/i18n-bundle-surface.mjs` (the constant and the predicate), and the files that spell the suffix beside it:
      - `scripts/pm/dispatch-gates.self-test.mjs`, on nine lines across several cases (lines 5641 to 5691, and 5881). One of them asserts the suffix on the live walk (`liveForms.every((f) => f.endsWith('.form.ts'))`, line 5662), so it fails as soon as the modules are renamed and is re-spelled with them;

### D3 — The name space: `OBJECT.KEY`, the served name, unchanged {#d3}

- **Grammar.** A `form` name is the dot-required qualified name the view item uses today (`packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts#ViewItemNameSchema`). The part before the last segment **must equal `object`**; a mismatch is refused at parse. The view item never enforced that equality; the new type does from its first release.
- **Why this name.** It is the name every form is served under today — a stored item's own `name`, and for a container's form the name `expandViewContainer` gives it. An action's `target`, a `/forms/:name` link, a `.custom` override's key and the withdrawal judgement's identity are all this string. Keeping it means the migration renames nothing, and a withdrawal recorded against a name keeps closing that name ([D6](#d6)). Under ADR-0150 D1 it is an object-derived literal name, the bucket `view` is in; `form` takes the same bucket and no (package, name) resolution.
- **Separate from lists.** After the split a list `crm_lead.all` and a form `crm_lead.all` are items of two types and do not collide. Until the old shapes are tombstoned they still share the `view` type's name space wherever the old shapes are stored, and the container name-collision refusal keeps judging them there. From card ②, and for as long as rule 11's replacement check is kept ([D5](#d5)), one write is refused, as an exception to that sentence: a package-less list is not saved at a name a read-only package authors as a form, as an item or through a container's expansion ([D5](#d5)). A list in a package is another item beside the form from the first release.
- **One form per name per package.** Inside a base's dependency closure ADR-0146 D2's rule applies unchanged: an undeclared same-name item of a dependent is refused at publish.
- **The public address (slug) is a second identity**, declared in `sharing.publicLink`. Two different forms on one slug are not refused today. By [ruled question 3](#q3) the `form` write door, `os validate` and publish refuse it.

The name is ruled ([question 2](#q2)); the alternative not taken is a short name addressed by package.

### D4 — Overrides: `form` joins ADR-0146 D2's list {#d4}

`form` is overridable by declaration: an item of the base's paired `.custom` that carries the base form's name and `overrides: { package, basedOn }` replaces the base's form whole (ADR-0146 D2, D8). This is what Setup's on/off switch writes and what the designer writes when it edits an installed app's form.

- **The classification row** is `form: listed` in `OVERRIDE_TYPE_CLASSIFICATION`, basis `adr_0146_d2_listed`, no conditions. `packages/spec/src/kernel/customization-package.test.ts` fails when a registry type has no row there and when a row names no registry type, so the row lands in the **same change** as the registry row — card ②. This record changes no code.
- **Whole-document replace stands.** The switch-only override Setup writes is the base's whole document with one key changed; `ui/packages/app-shell/src/views/setup/publicForms.ts#overrideHoldsOnlySwitch` is how the page tells such an override from a redesign.
- **`form` is not a security-catalog type** (ADR-0146 D6): the anonymous grant is derived from the resolved form's object at the door (ADR-0138 D2), not held by name in the security catalog. What an override may do to anonymous intake is bounded by [D6](#d6), not by excluding the type.

### D5 — Adding the type is additive, with one write narrowed: one projection reads the three old shapes {#d5}

The release that adds the type is major 19 (`7142585849`, rulings 1 and 2). What follows is the state of `main` between the cards' merges, and of no release; after the major the projection serves no content, and only the kept reader and rule 11's check remain ([D7](#d7), "After the major"). Two things here last longer, and say so where they stand:

- **The projection.** One pure function in `packages/spec` — beside `expandViewContainer`, built on it — turns a `view` body into the `form` items it carries: the container's `form`, each `formViews` entry, and a `viewKind: 'form'` item's `config`. Each projected item takes the name it is served under today, `object` from the old four-rung resolution, and the body unchanged. It is the **only** reader of the old shapes for form content: the loader, the stored-row read, the anonymous doors, the withdrawal judgement and the lint all call it, and none re-reads `formViews` by hand.
- **For the served `form` read the projection reads served rows only, and "served" is asked at each package's address.** A stored `view` row enters the effective `form` read through the projection only where the served-row order serves that row today. The order is asked per package address, inside the `form` read's own resolution over both types (the bullet "A stored old-shape row keeps replacing its base's form", below). ⛔ It is never taken from the effective `view` list. One row can be the served row at one package's address and be passed over at another's, at the same name: a package-less row is in every package's order. A row passed over at an address is projected into no served read at that address, in any binding; it stays in whichever withdrawal layer reads it today, where a row may only narrow ([D6](#d6) rules 4 and 5).
  - ⛔ **Two implementations this rules out.** One that projects every stored `view` row serves forms no door answers today. One that takes its served rows from the effective `view` list loses a row that still stands over a read-only package's form: once that package authors the form in `forms:` it has left the `view` type, the `view` merge no longer resolves its address, and the package's source `form` item would be served in place of the row that keeps the address closed.
  - **The case this decides: a package-less `view` row that carries an open form, behind a writable package's own `view` row that carries a list of the same name.** Today the package's row takes the slot at the package's address, the read there serves the list, no layer reads the package-less row, and both doors answer `404`. From card ②'s release the answer is the same: the package-less form is not served. It is not served because the row is passed over, and not because the list is read as written over a form: the list is the package's own item and closes nothing of its package's.
  - **This is the old shapes' shared name space, not a new collision.** [D3](#d3) keeps a list and a form of one name apart as items of two types, and says the old shapes still share the `view` type's name space wherever they are stored. Both rows here are `view` rows. So the rule also holds for a list saved into a writable package after card ② at the name of a package-less old-shape form: at that package's address the list is served and the package-less row is passed over, as today. Where no read-only package's address serves the package-less row, the save closes its form. Where the row is the served row at a read-only package's address, because it stands over that package's form, it stays the served body of that form there, whatever a writable package holds at the name: a link-less row that keeps a base's address closed keeps it closed after the list is saved. A form authored or stored as a `form` item is not passed over by a `view` row of any name.
  - **Cost.** The cost is nothing a door answers today. The rows cannot be told from two items their administrator means to keep, so nothing moves the form: [D7](#d7), D7-d says what the ceremony does with the pair, and by the ruling recorded beside [question 4](#q4) the row stays as stored.
  - **Pin card ② owes, with an ablation.** A writable package's own `view` row that carries a list, and a package-less `view` row of the same name that carries an open form: both doors answer `404` at the form's address before card ②'s change and after it (ablate: project every stored `view` row into the `form` read, and the address answers). A second fixture, for the address: a read-only package authors an open `form` item, and a package-less `view` row of that name whose form has no link stands over it; a list of the name is then saved into an app of the environment's own, and into the pair as its own item. Both doors answer `404` at the package's address before the list is saved and after it (ablate: take the served rows from the effective `view` list, and the address answers).
- **`form` reads serve authored items and projected ones.** A projected item is marked as such in its read envelope so a surface can say "defined in a view" and offer the move.
- **An authored `form` item shadows a projected one of the same name in the same package, inside one layer.** A package's source `form` item shadows the projection of that package's own source; a stored `form` row shadows the projection of a stored row at the same address. It never shadows a withdrawal ([D6](#d6)). ⛔ A package's source item never shadows the projection of a **stored** row: stored over source is the layering order whichever type each side is written in (next bullet).
- **A stored old-shape row keeps replacing its base's form when the base moves to `form`.** Today a stored `view` row that stands over a package's form replaces it whole: a declared override in the pair, a row bound to the package, a package-less row, or a stored container whose expansion writes the name. The merge that does it runs inside one type (`packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-overlay-merge.ts#mergePackageAwareOverlay`). A package may move its source to `forms:` while the stored row is still a `view` row, and card ④ does exactly that to first-party apps. So the effective `form` read resolves a package's form over both types, in the one served-row order (`packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-overlay-merge.ts#servedOverlayRowCandidates`): the stored `view` row that order picks at the package's address is served, as the `form` item it projects, over the package's source `form` item of that name — as it is served over the package's old-shape form today. A stored `form` row at the same place in the order wins over the stored `view` row, and the `form` write of the bullet below removes the latter. A stored `view` row that carries no form at the name is the next bullet's. [D6](#d6) rule 11 says what this protects.
- **A stored `view` row that carries no form at a form's name is read as written over the form only in the bindings where no write can put it there beside the form.** [D3](#d3) makes a list and a form of one name two items, so a list row closes nothing by sharing a form's name. Inside the one type it does today: a row at a package's address replaces whatever the package ships under the name. The `form` read keeps that answer — nothing is served for the name from that package for as long as the row stands and rule 11's replacement check is kept — for three bindings and for no other row. Two are closed by a write door that exists today; the third is closed by a refusal card ② adds:
  - a **declared override** of the package. The override write gate refuses a declaration of an item its base does not ship in that type (`declared_package_ships_no_such_item`, `packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-packaged-base-refusal.ts#judgeOverrideWrite`), so a `view` override was declared while the package shipped the name as a `view` item;
  - a row **bound to a read-only package**. Once the package ships the name as a `form` item and not as a `view` item, a `view` save that names the package at that name is a new item in a read-only package, and that is refused (ADR-0070 D1, `WRITABLE_PACKAGE_REQUIRED`). So a row of this binding at the name was stored while the package shipped the name as a `view` item: the class `packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-packageless-overlay-migration.ts` carries as rows bound to a read-only base. This is exact for the name after the move, and only there. Before the move the close of saves that name a read-only package leaves a base with no pair open; a row saved through that corner was saved over the form, which is the reading;
  - a **package-less row**: an item, or a stored container whose expansion writes the name. ⛔ No door of today closes this binding for a form's name. ADR-0146 D11's close (`packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-packaged-base-refusal.ts#packagelessOverlayWriteRefusal`) refuses a package-less write only where its ceremony would move the row, and by its contract leaves the rest open: a name several read-only packages ship, a base whose pair cannot be formed or ensured, a container-shaped body, a row whose pair already holds the item. The container name-collision refusal in `packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-save.ts` judges the names a package ships as `view` items. Neither judges a name the package ships as a `form` item. **So card ② adds one refusal of its own, kept as long as the reading it protects**: every package-less `view` write that would store a list at a name a read-only package **authors** as a `form` item is refused.
    - **What it covers.** A save, the publish of a draft, and a rollback to a version that carries a list at the name; of an item, and of a container whose expansion writes the name, judged on the expansion as the readers place it (the way the container name-collision refusal judges one). It does not ask the state of the base's pair or how many packages ship the name. A re-save of a row already stored is refused too; the stored row keeps its bytes. A delete is not judged: it stores nothing.
    - **What triggers it.** The name is one a read-only package authors as a `form` item, in `forms:`. A form the projection reads out of a package's old shape does not trigger it: there the name is still a `view` item's, and today's doors judge the write as they do now.
    - **It refuses saves that are admitted today.** Where a package's form is an old-shape `view` item, a package-less list saves at its name today in the cases ADR-0146 D11's close leaves open: several read-only packages ship the name; the base has no pair that can be formed or ensured; a re-save of a stored row whose pair already holds the item; a container that overlays the package's own container by its own name, which neither the close nor the container name-collision refusal judges. Once the package authors the name as a `form` item, each of these is refused. An administrator who holds such a list can no longer edit it in place; a container is refused at every re-save until the member is renamed. That narrows what an administrator can save. It is small and it is meant: the list keeps a door (the next sub-bullet), a save that carries a form at the name is untouched, and the alternative is the corner the refusal closes — a list saved after the move that the read cannot tell from one written over the form. So card ② is `Metadata-protocol: narrowed` ([Execution plan](#execution-plan-after-acceptance)). The refusal reads a name only from the release in which a package authors it as a `form` item: for first-party apps that is card ④'s, for any other app its author's own.
    - **How it answers.** It sits beside the container name-collision refusal in the save door and answers with that refusal's envelope (`VALIDATION_ERROR`, `400`); no new `code`. The message names the form and the two saves that work whether or not the base has a pair: another key, or the list saved into an app of the environment's own. ⛔ It never prescribes a save under a name another stored row holds.
    - **What it does not cover.** A package-less `view` write that carries a form at the name is an old-shape write of the form and saves as before (two bullets down); so does a rollback to a version that carries a form. A write that names a package is not package-less.
  - **So a list saved at a form's name after the package moved the form is never read as written over it.** Saved into a package — an app of the environment's own, or the base's pair as the pair's own item with no `overrides` — it is a list beside the form ([D3](#d3)), as an item or as a container's expansion alike: it is not in the base's served-row order, the form stays served, and the row is in no class that holds the upgrade ([D7](#d7)). Saved package-less it is refused. A row in a writable package is that package's own item and is never read as standing over that package's own form. A read-only package's own source is read the same way: a list its `views:` container expands at the name of its own `form` item is its list beside its form.
  - **Known limit, fail-closed.** A package-less row that carries a list at the name and was stored before this refusal judged the write — before the package shipped anything under the name, or through a corner today's doors leave open — cannot be told from one written over the form. It is read the same way, an item and a container's expansion alike. That is today's answer inside one type too: a package that ships an item under a name an environment row already holds is served that row. The cost is availability in that one deployment: the package's form is not served there, the form's layered read names the row that stands over it, and the row gets no disposition of its own: it stays as stored and does not hold the upgrade (the ruling recorded beside [question 4](#q4)).
  - **The reading and the refusal last as long as rule 11's replacement check, past major 19.** By the ruling recorded beside [question 4](#q4), a row of this reading is not moved and does not hold major 19, so it reaches the major as stored. What keeps its base's address closed there is the replacement check this record keeps ([D7](#d7), "After the major"), and the bounded reading stays with it. The refusal stays too: without it a package-less list saved after the major at a form's name could not be told from a row written over the form. Card ⑤ removes neither; both leave in the release that removes the check.
  - **Pins card ② owes for this bullet, each with an ablation.**
    - (a) The bound. Over a package that authors an open `form` item: a list saved package-less at the form's name is refused (status and `code`); a package-less container whose expansion writes a list at that name is refused; both are refused where the base's pair cannot be ensured. The same list saved into an app of the environment's own, and into the pair as its own item, saves. After all of them the two doors serve the package's form at its address. Ablate the bound (let the reading take any stored `view` row of the name): the doors answer `404`. Ablate the expansion half of the refusal: the container saves, and the doors answer `404`.
    - (b) The closed reading. A package-less `view` row that carries a list at the name, seeded as stored, keeps both doors at `404` at the package's address; so does a stored package-less container whose expansion writes a list there (ablate: fall through to the package's source `form` item when the stored row projects no form). If card ②'s measurement ([D7](#d7), D7-b) finds a door that admits a declared override carrying no form, this pin gains that row as a fixture in the same change.
- **`view` reads are unchanged** until the major: `GET /api/v1/meta/view` keeps listing `viewKind: 'form'` items, and `GET /api/v1/meta/view/:name` keeps answering a form's name. They are frozen: they serve the old shapes only and are not the picture of an environment's forms once a `form` item exists. Every platform reader moves to `form` — the anonymous doors in card ②, the console in card ③.

- **A `form` write acts on the form whichever type stores it.** A save, reset or delete of a `form` at `(package, name)` also removes the old-shape row that carries the same form at the same address, in the same transaction: an item-shaped `view` row is deleted, a stored container is re-saved without that form. One address holds one definition of a form; the Setup switch written before the re-point (a `view` override) is found and cleared by the re-pointed switch. ⛔ It removes form content only. ### D6 — The anonymous doors and the withdrawal judgement read both types {#d6}

This is the security-sensitive part. ADR-0138 D2 row 1 is unchanged: the admission condition is still the form's own `sharing` declaration, and the grant is still insert and read-back on the form's one object. What changes is where the declaration is read from. The rules:

1. **One resolution.** Every anonymous door keeps reading the form through the one resolution in `registerFormEndpoints`. No door gets its own copy of "is this form public".
2. **One candidate rule.** `anonymousFormIntakeCandidates` gains the `form` item as a fourth shape (the item itself is the form). The three-part open test does not change.
3. **Candidates come from the effective `form` read** — authored items and projections ([D5](#d5)).
4. **The withdrawal layer is both types, every layer.** A candidate named `N` is served only when no body that carries `N` explicitly withdraws it, across: the effective list of `form`, the effective list of `view`, and the per-package layer of each. Old-shape bodies are passed through the projection first, so a container's `formViews` entry is judged under the name it is served as.
5. **Narrowing only, in both directions.** A `form` item cannot re-open a form an old-shape body withdraws, and an old-shape body cannot re-open a form a `form` item withdraws. The shadowing of [D5](#d5) selects which body is *served*; it never removes a body from the withdrawal layer.
6. **Identity is the served name, and inside one name the whole item.** A body named `N` that explicitly withdraws a form closes `N`. For a `form` body the item is the slot, and after rule 4 an old-shape body is judged as the items it projects, one form each. The match does not ask the slug: a withdrawn form does not escape by pointing its link at a new slug, because the body of its name still withdraws it. A definition under **another name** is a different form and a withdrawal of the first name does not close it. That is today's rule, in the module's header: the doors "judge by the served item name", and "Another row that publishes or withdraws the same slug is a different form and closes nothing". This record does not widen identity to the slug across names. What keeps a second form off a switched-off form's address is [ruled question 3](#q3)'s refusal at the write door, where a switched-off form that keeps its link still holds it. The known limit recorded in the header (a withdrawal of a name closes that name in every package) is kept, because it fails closed.
   - **The slug match of `anonymousFormIntakeWithdrawnIn` decides no case in this layer, and no pin is asked for it.** It decides exactly one case: two bodies of one name whose forms sit under different members, which only a container handed over as stored can be. Rule 4 leaves no such body in the doors' layer, and a container the projection cannot turn into items is closed whole by rule 7. The function keeps the match for its callers outside this layer, which may be handed a container body as stored (the major-18 ceremony in `packages/cli/src/utils/organization-ownership-apply.ts`, and Setup's judgement of an app's own document until card ③ re-points it); it leaves with the old shapes.
7. **A body that cannot be read closes what it names.** A stored `view` body that carries form content the projection cannot turn into items is not skipped: every public link it names is treated as withdrawn, and the boot says so once at `error`, naming the row and the ceremony of [D7](#d7).
8. **A switched-off form answers `404 FORM_NOT_FOUND`**, as a form that does not exist (ruling record 7100386248, F3 = A). The tenancy intake rule is unchanged and runs after the match.
9. **Write-time judgements cover `form`.** The tenancy intake advisory and the organization-scoped refusal of a save that would leave a withdrawn form open judge a `form` write as they judge a `view` write, over the same two-type layer.
10. **The reader of old-shape withdrawals outlives the old shapes.** See [D7](#d7) and [ruled question 4](#q4).
11. **Replacement is honoured across the two types.** Not every closed address is closed by a withdrawal. A stored row whose body carries no link — the link cleared, or the `sharing` block removed — over a base whose form declares an open one closes the address by replacing the base's body: an override's body replaces its slot, and the doors take candidates from the effective list only. `packages/rest/src/form-routes.ts` says it in words: "the base's form the override removed — is not offered". That is not an explicit withdrawal (`anonymousFormExplicitWithdrawals` counts a body only when it keeps a link), so rules 4 and 5 do not hold it. What holds it is [D5](#d5)'s resolution: the stored row stays the served body of its base's form in the effective `form` read, whichever type the base and the row are each written in. A package upgrade that moves the base's form to `forms:` opens nothing a stored row had closed, and neither does [D7](#d7)'s migration.

**Rule 7's boot line, as revised (`7142585849`, ruling 2). It is written here, outside the rules, and the rules stand as written.** The line names the row, the links it closed and the repair. It names the ceremony once the ceremony card has landed: the ceremony ships with the major and its upgrade command runs it, so `main` before that card has no ceremony to name.

**Pins card ② owes, each with an ablation** (remove the named read, watch the pin turn red, restore):

- a form withdrawn by a `view` override in `.custom`, with an authored `form` item of the same name open: both doors answer `404` (ablate: the `view` half of the layer);
- the mirror: withdrawn by a `form` override, open as an old-shape item in the base (ablate: the `form` half);
- withdrawn in a container's `formViews` entry, open as a `form` item named for the expansion (ablate: the projection step of the layer);
- withdrawn in a body of the same name whose link names another slug than the open definition's: both doors answer `404` at the open definition's address (ablate: the whole-item match of rule 6 — make it require an equal slug);
- a per-package-layer withdrawal in either type (ablate: `getMetaItemsPerPackage` for that type);
- an unreadable old-shape body closes its links (ablate: rule 7);
- **replacement across the types** (rule 11): a stored `view` override in `.custom` whose body has no `publicLink`, over a base that authors the same name as an open `form` item, and its twin with a package-less row: both doors answer `404` at the base's address (ablate: the cross-type resolution of [D5](#d5) — serve the package's source `form` item when the stored row is a `view` row);
- the submit door refuses where the resolve door does, on each case above.

Each pin asserts the refusal's status and `code` and that no record was written, not a bare status.

### D7 — The old shapes retire in the next protocol major, with their migration {#d7}

**Schedule.** The protocol is at major 18 (`packages/spec/src/kernel/protocol-version.ts#PROTOCOL_VERSION`). The three old shapes are tombstoned in **major 19** and not before: `ViewSchema.form`, `ViewSchema.formViews`, and the `form` member of `packages/spec/src/ui/view.zod.ts#ViewKindSchema` with the `form` arm of `ViewItemSchema` and `ViewItemWireSchema`. The type is added in the same major (`7142585849`, rulings 1 and 2). Each tombstone carries its prescription ("a form is a `form` item: move it to `forms:` …") as `retiredKey()` and `enumWithRetiredValues` do today. `Metadata-protocol: narrowed`. From that release `view` reads serve lists only.

**Sources — an ADR-0087 conversion and `os migrate meta`.**

- One D2 conversion, `toMajor: 19`, on the stack: it moves each named form out of `views[]` into `forms[]` under the projection's name, lifts the binding to `object`, and leaves the lists where they are. A container left with no member is removed. It is lossless for every form it moves.
- It is the first conversion that moves an item between two stack collections; its fixture pairs cover the three shapes, a container holding both lists and forms, an item that declares `overrides` with no link in a record-form slot (moved, D7-b), and a form whose `data.object` disagrees with its container (left in place, with a structured TODO).
- **Its output is judged by [question 3](#q3)'s refusal like any `form`.** One arrangement that is served today does not pass it: a retired form that keeps its link, switched off, beside its successor open on the same address under another name. Once both are `form` items the retired one still holds the address, so `os validate` and the successor's next save are refused, naming both. The conversion moves both forms as written and chooses neither; `os migrate meta` lists the pair as an edit for the author, and the way out is the one in the message: clear the retired form's link. The ceremony moves the stored twins the same way, as a move and not a save: what the doors serve does not change (the successor stays open, the retired form stays closed), its report names the pair, and the successor's next save is refused until the link is cleared.
- **Internal record forms are not moved** (ruling item 3). An internal record form is a form in a record-form slot — the container's default `form`, `formViews.default`, `formViews.create`, an item with `isDefault: true` or named `OBJECT.create` — that declares **no `sharing.publicLink`**, that no `type: 'form'` action in the stack targets, and that does **not stand over a base form that declares a link** (D7-b, two bullets down). The slots are the ones `form-view-internal-not-loaded` names. The test for "public" is [D1](#d1)'s and ⛔ not that rule's: the rule asks `sharing.enabled === true` (`isPublicForm` in `packages/lint/src/validate-internal-form-views.ts`), which is right for its warning and wrong here, because it reads a switched-off public form as internal. The conversion deletes the internal ones and says so per form; their layout belongs to the object's `fieldGroups` (HotCRM's own are objectstack-ai/hotcrm#2739's).
- **(D7-a) A form that declares a `sharing.publicLink` is never internal — switched on or off, in whatever slot.** It is moved with its `sharing` block as written, the switch included, by the conversion and by the ceremony alike. So is a form an action opens. The reason is the kill switch: a body that keeps its link with a switch set to `false` is an explicit withdrawal (`anonymousFormExplicitWithdrawals`), and it is what Setup's switch-off writes for a default-slot form too (the base's whole document with one key changed, `isDefault` included). Deleting such a body deletes the withdrawal. In a package's source the per-package layer would stop closing the form and an open override of the same name would be served; in a stored row the app's own open form would re-open. [Ruled question 4](#q4) forbids that direction, and ADR-0087 D2 admits only a lossless conversion. A form moved out of a record-form slot loses only its record-form role (`isDefault` is not a `form` key, [D2](#d2)), which no surface has read since #36425.
- **(D7-b) A form that stands over a base form is classed by its base, never by its own body alone.** A stored row, or an item of a `.custom` source, stands over a base when the read serves it in place of a package's own form of the same name: a declared override in the pair, a row bound to the base package, a package-less overlay row, a stored container whose expansion writes the name. Where the base's form declares a `sharing.publicLink`, that row is never internal, whatever its own body declares and whatever slot it sits in: no link, a link to another address, a link with a switch off. It **moves with its base**, as the override it is and with its body as written (a body with no link stays without one), by the conversion and by the ceremony alike. Nothing deletes it and nothing prescribes deleting it. The reason is rule 11 of [D6](#d6): such a row is what keeps the base's address closed today, although its own body holds no withdrawal. Deleting or resetting it serves the base's body again, and the public address answers.
  - **The source conversion cannot read another package's source, so it does not ask the base**: a form held by an item or a container that carries an `overrides` declaration is moved to `forms[]` whatever its body and slot, with the declaration.
    - **This goes past ruling item 3's words. It is this record's own reading, which the maintainer accepts with the record or strikes** (TL;DR 10). Ruling item 3 says the record create and edit form "does not become a `form` item". Where the base's form of that name is an internal record form, the moved item is the override of one, and the conversion's output holds it in `forms[]`. This record reads item 3 as the rule on what `form` content is ([D1](#d1)), and that rule does not move: nothing renders the moved item on a record, because the type has no key that binds a form to a record page ([D2](#d2)). The item is carried across only because the conversion cannot see the base, and the wrong guess in the other direction opens a public address.
    - **It does not stay: `os validate` refuses it.** `os validate` sees both packages. An item that declares an override of a form its base ships in neither type is an error and not a warning — the grade the override write gate gives today to a declaration of an item its base does not ship (`declared_package_ships_no_such_item`, `packages/metadata-protocol/src/protocol-packaged-base-refusal.ts#judgeOverrideWrite`). "Ships" is read over both types, through the projection, so an override of a base that still authors an old shape passes. The message names the base and the two ways out: remove the item (a record form's layout belongs to the object's `fieldGroups`), or correct the name. Only there is a removal offered, and only because the base ships no form of that name, so no address depends on the item. `os migrate meta` lists it as an edit for the author. It is a refusal so that a record form's override cannot stay among the `form` items for good.
    - **The alternative not taken**: leave such an item where it is, in `views[]` with a structured TODO, as the `data.object` disagreement is left, and report it. Major 19 refuses the old shape, so an item left in place is out of the source until its author acts. Where the base's form is linked, the override that kept the address closed is then not shipped and the base's form is served — the item this record protects is the one the alternative drops. If the maintainer strikes the reading, this is the fallback, and it needs its own answer to that case before card ⑤ builds it.

Whether a write door admits a declared override whose body carries no form over a form was not measured for this record; card ② measures it. A package-less row of the class needs no such door: a list that held the name before the package shipped anything under it is one ([D5](#d5)'s known limit). Rule 11 keeps the address closed in dual-read either way. For the migration the row gets no disposition of its own, by the ruling recorded beside [question 4](#q4): the ceremony writes nothing for it, the precheck does not hold major 19 for it, and it stays as stored. Its base's address stays closed after the major by rule 11's replacement check, which this record keeps ("After the major"). - The translation tails move with them ([D8](#d8)), by a second conversion on `translations`.
- A D3 chain entry for major 19 carries the semantic half; `os migrate meta --from 18` lists the edits and `--write` applies the ones it can prove.

**Stored rows — a ceremony, because the stored pass cannot change a row's type.** `applyConversionsToStoredItem` reads back the collection of the row's own type, so the move from a `view` row to a `form` row is a migration of rows, in the shape ADR-0131 D10 and ADR-0146 D11 set: a ceremony the major's upgrade command runs: convert first, then switch (`7142585849`, ruling 2). An administrator can take the read-only run before upgrading. The write is reached only from the upgrade command, and the standalone command is read-only (a seat decision, addendum `7142658348`). - A **`.custom` override** of a form keeps its package, its name and its `overrides` declaration, `basedOn` unchanged: it is the same override in the type its base now has. This covers every switch Setup has written, and every override that differs from its base in any other way, a cleared link included: an override is never set aside as an internal record form on its own body (D7-b).
- A **package-less overlay row** over a packaged form (ADR-0146 D11's unmigrated class) is moved to the destination D11 gives it: a declared `form` override in the base's pair, with its body as written — its switch when it has one, and no link when it has none (D7-b).

## Consequences

- **Authors and AI authors** get one place for a form (`forms:`) and one for lists (`views:`). A public form no longer hides inside a list container, and its object is a key on the form.
- **`view` gets simpler in major 19**: one kind, two container members.
- **Two readers of form content exist on `main` only, until the major-19 card** (the type and the projection; `7142585849`, rulings 1 and 2: no release carries both). The cost is bounded by the rule that the projection is the only reader of the old shapes.
- **The anonymous doors read two types on `main` until the major-19 card, and keep the second type's withdrawal reader after it**, as D7-f says (`7142585849`, rulings 1 and 2). [D6](#d6)'s pins are the price; a withdrawal is honoured in both.
- **The major-19 upgrade runs the ceremony**, and refuses to switch only for a row it cannot move (`7142585849`, ruling 2). A stored row whose customization pair cannot be ensured holds the upgrade until the cause the precheck names is removed. Where the cause is the App's own manifest and the administrator does not author the App, the deployment stays on major 18 until the App ships a fix or is uninstalled ([D7](#d7)).
- **A stored row that keeps a public address closed is never set aside as an internal record form**, whether it closes by a switch or by replacing its base's form. The migration moves it, or by the later ruling leaves it as stored, and no report or precheck offers its removal ([D6](#d6) rule 11, [D7](#d7)). Where a form row stands behind a no-form row, removing the row in front before the major serves the row behind it and not the base's form; the report names both. The price is one more read rule for the dual-read period, and a `.custom` source's overrides being moved by the conversion without a look at the base. `os validate` then refuses the one whose base ships no form of that name, so a record form's override does not stay among the `form` items (this record's reading of ruling item 3, [D7](#d7)).
- **The ceremony can switch off a public form that is open today.** Where a stored row no layer reads today withdraws a form — a package-less row behind a writable package's own row, or behind a row bound to the base — the fold carries the withdrawal, and the form answers `404` after `--apply`. The report names it as newly closed, and the administrator switches it on again in Setup ([D7](#d7), D7-d). Where several stored rows of one form exist, the one whose body the read serves today is the one kept.
- **From card ②, and for as long as rule 11's replacement check is kept, a package-less list is not saved at a name a read-only package authors as a form**, as an item or through a container's expansion. It is a refusal card ② adds and an exception to [D3](#d3) that outlasts major 19. **Some of these saves are admitted today**: where several packages ship the name, where the base has no pair, a re-save of a row its pair already holds, and a container that overlays the package's own container. An administrator who holds such a list can no longer edit it in place once the package authors the form. The door that remains: another key, or the list saved into an app of the environment's own. Card ② is `narrowed` for it.
- **Two kinds of stored row get no disposition of their own** (ruled; [question 4](#q4), the last paragraph after it). A package-less form that no layer reads today, behind a writable package's own list of the same name, stays unread: it is not served from card ②'s release and the ceremony does not move it. A row that carries no form over a linked base — a package-less list that held the name before the package shipped a form under it, say — keeps that form unserved in that deployment for as long as rule 11's replacement check is kept. Both stay as stored, and major 19 is not held for them. Serving the base's form in such a deployment is the administrator's own act on the row; no report prescribes it ([D5](#d5), [D7](#d7)).
- **One arrangement served today is refused once it is in the new type**: a retired form that keeps its link beside its successor on the same address. The author clears the retired form's link ([question 3](#q3), this record's reading of "holds").
- **`defineForm` changes meaning** in card ② ([ruled question 1](#q1)): a declared, compiler-carried export break, on the premise that its outside use is platform-internal. The 17 Studio form modules change file suffix in the same card ([D2](#d2), the seat's routing).
- **First-party work**: about 100 non-test source files and about 160 test files name the old shapes; they move in ②, ③ and ④ and the remainder falls to ⑤.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep forms in `view` and retire only the container shape.** On the decision card; not taken by the maintainer.
- **Keep everything as is.** On the decision card; not taken.
- **A separate `public_form` type, leaving action-opened forms in `view`.** Not taken: two types for one schema, and ruling item 1 names both consumers as `form` content.
- **Tombstone the old shapes in a minor.** Not taken: the card fixes the tombstone to a major, and HotCRM and stored rows carry the shapes today.
- **Migrate stored rows through the read-seam conversion pass.** Not possible: that pass cannot change a row's type ([Context](#loading-overriding-translating)).
- **Make the record create and edit form a `form` item with a default role.** Refused by ruling item 3.
