## TL;DR

- An object may declare **`closedStates`**: for one or more of its state fields, the values in which a record of that object is closed. Nothing is stored for it; a record is closed when its stored state says so.
- A closed record **and its direct `master_detail` lines** are closed to change. This is the same unit the pending-approval lock holds.
- **Every caller is bound**, at every door, whatever grants it holds, and so is a write that names no principal. The state field itself is frozen with the rest. ▲ **A system context is not bound**: the platform's own services, and the app's own elevated automation (an action body, a flow or hook that runs as system). That automation is how a closed document is reopened or moved on.
- A new approval stays a way out of a state that is closed on the approval's own status field. **An abandoned approval never writes a closed value** (ruled 2026-11-26): a document the approval opened stays open. ▲ **An approval opens on such a field only where the field can show it in progress**: the field declares `pending`, the object closes on neither `pending` nor `returned`, every step of the approval that can act on the object mirrors onto that one field, and nothing the platform can read ahead would stop or hold the approval between two steps. Otherwise its first request is refused: the platform refuses the configuration that would leave a document reading a decision its approval did not finish (ruled 2026-11-26).
- ▲ **A closed record is not deleted by a caller**, and a delete whose cascade would reach a closed record or a line of one is refused whole, before anything is removed.
- ▲ The lock is judged on **the row as stored when the write reaches it**. The write that closes a record lands whole; the next one is refused. Item ③ of the batch stays the rule for `readonlyWhen` and does not govern this lock (ruled 2026-11-26).
- The refusal is the one clients already handle, **`409 RECORD_LOCKED`**, with its own sentence. A caller whom the object's permission or the row's access refuses gets the answer an open record gives.
- ▲ **One lock read**, owned by the data layer, answers for both producers, and tells every caller who can read the record whether it is locked and which lock holds. The approvals-family read stays as published and answers for approvals only.
- The showcase's three paid-invoice hooks become one declaration. That conversion is the proof.

## Context

### What a document's life looks like, and where the platform stops today

A business document is drafted, submitted, decided, and then it is finished: an approved purchase order, a paid invoice, a posted journal, a closed period's timesheet. After that point the document is evidence. People still read it, and automation may still move it on (a paid invoice is refunded; an approved order is cancelled by a controller), but nobody edits its amounts or swaps its lines by hand.

The platform holds a document through the first half of that life and lets go at the decision.

- **While an approval is pending**, the record is locked: an update is refused `409 RECORD_LOCKED` (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/lifecycle-hooks.ts#bindApprovalLockHook`), and so is adding, editing, removing or moving one of its `master_detail` lines (`#bindDetailRowLockHook`). That is ADR-0019-approval-as-flow-node's lock, extended to the lines by the 「一起锁」 ruling.
- **When the approval is decided**, the request is finalised and the lock is gone. The approvals guide says so twice in as many sections: "the record unlocks" (`content/docs/automation/approvals.mdx`). PR [#36135](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/pull/36135), which taught the lock read to answer for lines, says it in its own body: "Decided documents are not locked; edits after the decision are not in this PR."

What happens after the decision is the app author's job today, and the author has three tools, none of which says "this document is closed":

1. **`readonlyWhen` on each field.** A field whose predicate is true is stripped from a caller's update (`packages/objectql/src/validation/rule-validator.ts#stripReadonlyWhenFields`). It is a field lock: the author names every field one by one, a new field is unlocked until someone remembers it, and it cannot refuse a new line, a removed line or a delete.
2. **Display predicates on the built-in buttons.** `userActions.edit.disabledWhen` and `userActions.delete.visibleWhen` grey or hide a button. The spec says what they are: "advisory UI gating only" (`packages/spec/src/data/object.zod.ts`). The write itself is not judged.
3. **Hand-written hooks.** The showcase's invoice is the worked example. Its line object locks line fields with `readonlyWhen` predicates that read the header's status, and three hooks refuse adding, moving and removing a line under a paid invoice (`examples/app-showcase/src/data/hooks/index.ts#GuardPaidInvoiceLineAddHook`, `#GuardPaidInvoiceLineMoveHook`, `#GuardPaidInvoiceLineRemoveHook`). The header itself is covered by one `readonlyWhen` field and the two display predicates (`examples/app-showcase/src/data/objects/invoice.object.ts`).

Those hooks exist because of an earlier ruling. On [#24684](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/24684) the seat ruled "C, so B now" (record [`6281609466`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/24684#issuecomment-6281609466)): the showcase writes its own hooks now, and a declarative lock "returns only with a named producer beyond this showcase". Item ② of the 2026-11-22 batch is that return. It names the producers — approval, payment, closing — and says what they have in common: each only sets a state. The lock belongs to the state, declared once on the object, and not to whichever producer set it.

### The two locks that exist, and why neither is this one

| | Pending-approval lock | `readonlyWhen` |
|---|---|---|
| Holds | a record while a request on it is pending, and its lines | one field while its predicate is true |
| On a caller's write | refuses the write, `RECORD_LOCKED` | strips the field and lets the rest land |
| Who passes | a system context (`session.isSystem`); the flow run that opened the request; a write that carries only the approval's status mirror field | nobody: a system context does **not** pass, and only a value a hook derived inside the same write survives |
| Delete | the record's delete lands and cancels the request; a line's delete is refused | not judged |
| Readable by a client | yes: `getRecordLock` / `getRecordLocks` | through the field's own metadata |
| Ends | when the request is decided, recalled or cancelled | when the predicate turns false |

The pending lock has the right unit and the right refusal, but its producer is a request row that ends at the decision. `readonlyWhen` reads the right fact, the record's own state, but it is a field rule. The closed-state lock takes the unit and the refusal from the first and the fact from the second.

### The lock read that exists

A client can ask whether a record is locked before it offers an edit. The contract is `IApprovalService.getRecordLock` and its page form (`packages/spec/src/contracts/approval-service.ts#getRecordLock`, `#getRecordLocks`), answering `{ locked, lockSource?: 'record' | 'master' }`, or `null` when the caller cannot read the record. It is served by two approvals-family routes, composed into the record page's approvals part (`packages/rest/src/record-page-routes.ts#answerApprovals`), and read by the console's record page and grid (`ui/packages/app-shell/src/hooks/useRecordApprovals.ts`, `ui/packages/plugin-grid/src/hooks/useRowApprovalLocks.ts`). It lives in the approvals plugin because approvals was the only producer. With a second producer that the data layer itself enforces, a read that exists only when the approvals plugin is installed can no longer be the one read.

## Decision

### D1 — The key: `closedStates`

An object may declare, at object level, the states in which its records are closed:

```ts
// An invoice that is closed once paid or voided, or once its approval is decided.
closedStates: {
  status: ['paid', 'void'],
  approval_status: ['approved'],
}
```

- **Shape.** A map from a field of this object to the list of that field's values that close the record. A record is closed when **any** listed field holds one of its listed values ([D2](#d2--closed-is-read-off-the-stored-row-nothing-is-stored-for-it)). Two fields are the ordinary case, not an edge: an approval mirrors its outcome into one field and the business status lives in another, and the ruling's sentence "approval, payment and closing only set the state" is about exactly those producers.
- **What a field must be.** A stored, single-value `select` field of the object. Each listed value is one of that field's declared option values. A list is not empty and repeats nothing.
- **Refused where the object is authored.** A declaration that breaks one of those rules is a named refusal at parse and publish, the way `deleteBehavior: 'set_null'` on a `master_detail` is (`packages/spec/src/data/field.zod.ts`). So is removing or retyping a field, or removing an option, that a declaration still names. A closed-state declaration that cannot be evaluated never reaches a running app.
- **Optional, and absent means nothing.** An object without the key behaves exactly as it does today.
- **The line object declares nothing.** Being a `master_detail` line of a declaring object is what closes a line ([D3](#d3--the-unit-the-record-and-its-direct-master_detail-lines)). A line object may also declare its own `closedStates`, for a line that closes on its own.
- **No tie to a state machine.** The key does not require a `state_machine` rule on the field, and a `state_machine` rule does not imply the key. The rule says which moves are legal; the key says when the record stops accepting a caller's writes.
- **The name.** `closedStates`, not `lockedStates`: "locked" is the word the product already uses for a pending approval, where the lock releases by itself when the approval ends. A closed state does not end by itself.

For a metadata author this is a new key: the spec build card declares `Metadata-protocol: added`.

### D2 — Closed is read off the stored row; nothing is stored for it

A record is **closed** when, for at least one field its object's `closedStates` names, the stored value is one of the listed values. An empty state value closes nothing.

There is no closed flag, no lock row and no backfill. Two consequences follow, and both are intended:

- Whatever sets the state closes the record: an approval's status mirror, a payment service, an action, a caller's own edit of the state field where the app leaves that field writable. The producers "only set the state".
- Publishing a declaration closes every stored record already in a listed state, from that publish. Removing a value opens them again. Changing the declaration is an ordinary metadata change by whoever may author the object.

### D3 — The unit: the record and its direct `master_detail` lines

The unit a closed record holds is the record itself and every row, of any object, that points at it through a `master_detail` field. This is the unit of the pending-approval line lock (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/lifecycle-hooks.ts#bindDetailRowLockHook`), by the card's own words.

- **Every `master_detail` edge counts.** A junction row under two masters is a line of both; it is closed when either master is.
- **One level.** A line's own lines are not in the unit. If deeper levels are ever wanted, that is decided for both producers at once, so the unit stays one.
- **Not in the unit:** rows that reference the record through a `lookup`, and the record's satellites that live in their own objects (comments, attachments, shares, history). They keep their own rules. A file field **on** the record is a field of the record and is closed with it.

### D4 — What a closed unit refuses, and when it is judged

For a writer that [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not) binds:

| Write | On a closed record | On a line of a closed record |
|---|---|---|
| Create | not judged: there is no stored state yet | refused |
| Update | refused, whichever fields it carries, the state fields included | refused |
| Delete | refused ([D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)) | refused |
| Move a line to another master | — | refused when either end is closed: out of a closed master, or into one |

- **An update is judged by the keys it carries.** One that carries no field writes nothing and is not refused; any other is a change, whether or not its values differ from the stored ones. This is the pending lock's own rule.
- **Whole or nothing.** A write that names many rows — a predicate update or delete, a many-row create of lines — is refused whole when one row it reaches is closed or is a line of a closed record, before any row is written.
- **Asked of the store, not listed.** Closed is a predicate over a row's own stored columns. So "does this write reach a closed row" is one existence probe the store can answer: the write's own predicate, as the engine has composed it with the caller's row scope, joined to the closed predicate, and for a line object the same through a semi-join to the master. Prime Directive 5 asks for that shape first, and it is the shape the build takes. A predicate update on `main` reads its rows only when something asks for them (`packages/objectql/src/engine-update.ts#readPriorRows`), and this lock is not to become the reason every predicate write lists its rows. A store that cannot run the line probe as one statement is answered from that same read of the matched rows, taken once: the read that exists, not a second path built for parity. The engine's ceiling on a per-row fan-out is asked of that row set once it is read, as it is before per-row hooks run (`packages/objectql/src/engine-hook-dispatch.ts#assertBulkPerRowHookBudget`), and a set over the ceiling is refused before any row is judged or written. A many-row create of lines asks once, for the masters it names.
- **What cannot be judged is refused.** With the probe in place three cases remain: a predicate write on a store that cannot run the probe and whose matched rows pass that ceiling; a line write that names its master by something the engine cannot read as one id; and a read of the state that fails. Each is refused, and none is taken for open. The pending line lock is narrower: it refuses a write it cannot resolve only while some record of that master object is held by a locking request. This lock does not copy that condition. Its test is the declaration: the master object declares closed states.
- **One refusal is another gate's.** Where a create of a line, or a move, names a master the caller's own read door does not return, this lock does not answer for that master. Unless the lock refuses the write for the master the line is leaving, the reference gate refuses it, in that gate's words, exactly as under an open master ([D10](#d10--where-it-is-enforced-and-how-it-sits-beside-the-mechanisms-that-exist)). Every other "refused" in the table is `RECORD_LOCKED`.
- **Creating a record in a closed state is not this lock's question.** Whether a record may be born `paid` is the state field's own rule: `initialStates` on its `state_machine`, and item ④ of the batch.

▲ **The lock is judged on the row as stored when the write reaches it**, inside the unit of work the write runs in ([ADR-0034](./0034-transactional-writes-and-ambient-transaction.md)).

- The write that takes an open record into a closed state **lands whole**, with whatever other fields it carries. The record was open when the write began, and the same caller could have made the same edits one write earlier.
- The road **into** a closed state is not guarded here. Who may set `status` to `paid`, and from where, stays with the field's permissions, the `state_machine` rule and the app's own actions.
- Inside one unit of work, order matters: once the closing write has landed, a later write in the same unit meets a closed record. A save that edits lines and closes the document writes the lines first. The console's master-detail save puts the header first today (`ui/packages/plugin-form/src/masterDetailTx.ts`); its build card orders a closing header write last.

**This is where the record meets item ③ of the batch.** The ruling record words that item: "A lock that reads its own field: it holds if its condition is true before or after the write." The closed-state lock reads the state field and freezes it, so by those words it could be such a lock. It would then also hold on a write whose *result* is closed, and a caller's write that closes a record would be refused whole, as a write to a closed record is. This record could not scope a ruling, so it put the question to the maintainer, who answered it on 2026-11-26 (ruling record [`7200732942`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7200732942)): item ③ governs `readonlyWhen`, the field lock, and this lock is judged on the stored row. [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered) keeps the other reading, what it would have bought and what it would have cost. `readonlyWhen` keeps item ③'s rule, and an object that carries both locks gets both.

### D5 — Who is bound: every caller; a system context is not

**The test.** A write is bound unless it is made in a system context: the mark only server code can set, carried to the write as `isSystem` (`packages/objectql/src/engine-execution-plumbing.ts#buildSession`). The tree has three kinds of writer, and the test sorts all three.

- **A caller's context is bound, at every door.** The data API and its batch forms, a page or grid in the console, an API key, an AI agent acting for a principal, and automation that acts as the caller: a flow that runs as the user, which is a flow's default, and a hook that runs as the user or inherits the context of the caller's write, which is a hook's default.
- **A write with no principal and no system mark is bound.** A programmatic call that passes no context, and a schedule-started flow that runs as `user` and so has no user to run as, reach the engine with no session at all. No identity is not an elevated identity. The pending lock applies the same test, and so do the showcase's hooks. Code that means to write a closed document as the platform says so by running as system.
- ▲ **A system context is not bound** (below).

Three things follow for a bound writer.

- **No grant opens a closed record.** No permission set, no `modifyAllRecords`, no administrator role. The pending lock has no such exemption either; an administrator who must change a closed document uses the app's own action for it, as below.
- **The state field is frozen with the rest.** A caller cannot reopen a closed record by editing its state. If it could, the lock would be one edit deep.
- **Two passes of the pending lock do not carry over.** The pending lock also lets through the flow run that opened the request, and a write that carries only the approval's status mirror field. A closed state has no run that owns it, and its state field is frozen. So a write to the record by a node that follows an approval decision, in a flow that runs as the user, is refused once the approval's outcome is a declared closed state. Those nodes are the commonest automation to write a document just after it is decided, and [Consequences](#consequences) says what the author does about it. Where such a node stands between two approval steps, the platform refuses the approval before it starts, below.

▲ **Not bound: a write made in a system context**, the context no client can assert for itself.

- **The platform's own services**: the approval status mirror (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#mirrorStatusField`), summary recompute (`packages/objectql/src/engine-summaries-and-expansion.ts#recomputeSummaries`), retention, an import or seed that runs as system.
- **The app's own elevated automation**: an action body (`packages/runtime/src/action-execution.ts#buildActionExecutionContext`), and a flow or hook declared `runAs: 'system'` (`packages/spec/src/automation/flow.zod.ts#runAs`). An action's script body is in this class whoever clicks the button: the action's own gates decide who may run it, and the body then runs elevated. An action that updates declaratively, with no body, is not in it. The platform makes that write under the caller's own context, and it is bound like an edit.

This is how the card's sentence is met: "a record leaves the closed state only through a sanctioned action that moves it back". "Refund", "Void", "Reopen for correction" are actions the app ships, each with its own gate on who may run it; the action's body sets the state, and an open record is an ordinary record again. An app that ships no such action, and no system automation that moves the state, gives its users no way to reopen, and that is a legitimate product.

**One platform service moves a state with no app action behind it: the approval status mirror.** It writes a request's status onto the mirror field: `pending` when the request opens, then `approved`, `rejected`, `returned` or `recalled`. A state field has declared options ([D1](#d1--the-key-closedstates)), and onto such a field the mirror writes a status only where the options declare it. A status the field does not declare is not written, and the field keeps the value it holds; the one exception is a request that is abandoned, below (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#mirrorStatusField`, `packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#mirroredStatusValue`). The mirror writes in a system context, and four things of the object's own still meet its writes. A validation rule can refuse one, and so can a hook of the app. So can a `visibleWhen` on the option the mirror writes: the rule validator refuses a write of a value whose option's predicate is false on the record, in a system context as for a caller (`packages/objectql/src/validation/rule-validator.ts#evaluateOptionVisibility`). On `main` a refused write is logged and swallowed. A `readonlyWhen` on the field binds a system context too, and it does not refuse: it drops the value, the write lands without it, and the mirror sees no failure (`packages/objectql/src/validation/rule-validator.ts#stripReadonlyWhenFields`). So a new approval of a record that is closed on its mirror field opens the record where the mirror's `pending` lands, and leaves it closed throughout where it does not. The rule below makes the first a condition of the approval.

**An approval, in this record, is one passage of a record through its approval flow, taken as a whole**: the flow run that was started for the record, and every child run under that run, a `subflow` body or an item of a `map`, each of which runs under an id of its own. An approval can hold many requests: one for each approval step it reaches, in whichever of its runs, and one more each time a returned request is resubmitted. [ADR-0044](./0044-approval-send-back-for-revision.md) numbers those resubmissions as the rounds of a request, and this record leaves that word to it. Two things are a step's own and not the approval's. Each approval step names its own mirror field, or none, and every status of its request is written onto that field and onto no other (`packages/spec/src/automation/approval.zod.ts#approvalStatusField`, `packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#mirrorStatusField`). And each files its request on the record its run carries: in a `subflow` body that is the parent's record, and in a `map` body it is the item, where the items are records, on the object the `map` names for them (`packages/services/service-automation/src/builtin/map-node.ts`).

▲ **An approval opens on a closing field only where the field can show it in progress at every step, and where nothing the platform can read ahead would stop or hold it between two steps.** Ruled 2026-11-26 at 23:51Z (ruling record [`7205949718`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7205949718)): 「平台拒绝这种配置」, the platform refuses this configuration. The option the maintainer chose names a field that declares closed values and lacks an in-progress status or a way back, and an error at publish or when an approval opens. The conditions below are how this record keeps that rule, and [Points the ruling does not settle](#points-the-ruling-does-not-settle) lists what in them is this record's own or the seat's. The conditions concern an approval step whose mirror field is one on which the object closes on a status the mirror writes for a request that is not abandoned: `pending`, `returned`, `approved` or `rejected`. An object that closes on that field only on values of its own, `paid` or `void`, is asked nothing here: no approval leaves such a record closed on a decision it did not finish. Only these conditions are narrowed so. The rule for an abandoned request, below, holds for every closed value. For every step the conditions concern, all six hold:

- **The field declares `pending`, and the object closes on neither `pending` nor `returned`.** Those two are the statuses of a request in flight. A record under approval is held by the pending lock, which knows when to let go, in the revise window above all ([ADR-0044](./0044-approval-send-back-for-revision.md)). The closed-state lock does not.
- **Where a `state_machine` rule on the field declares the moves out of a closed `approved` or `rejected`, `pending` is among them** (`packages/objectql/src/validation/rule-validator.ts#checkStateMachine`). The move is asked of the closed values the mirror writes and of no other: a rule may keep a closed value of the object's own final.
- **The field carries no `readonlyWhen`, and no option of it for a request's status carries a `visibleWhen`.** On a record where its predicate holds, a `readonlyWhen` takes away the `pending` of the next request and every value the mirror would put back. A `visibleWhen` on the option for `pending`, `returned`, `approved`, `rejected` or `recalled` refuses the mirror's write of that status on a record where its predicate is false, and on `pending` that is the write that opens a closed record. The platform does not evaluate a predicate ahead to learn in which states it can hold, so it asks that the field carry no `readonlyWhen` and those options no `visibleWhen`. `readonly` is what keeps callers off a mirror field, and the mirror passes it.
- **Every approval step that can act on a record of the object mirrors onto that field.** A request's statuses are written onto its own step's field and onto no other, so a step that mirrors onto another field, or onto none, moves nothing on this one. After a step that left a closed outcome on the field, the record would stay on that outcome, closed, while the other step is open, and for good unless that step approves: through its rejection, through its being abandoned, and through a revise window that would open on a record this lock still holds. The steps ahead of a step the conditions concern are held to the field as the steps after it are. A decided document that is sent for approval again opens on the outcome the last approval left, so the first step of the new approval follows that decision as a later step follows an earlier one. The check cannot read ahead whether the items of a `map` are records, so a step in a `map` body counts as one that can act on the parent's record as well as on an item.
- **No flow of the approval that runs as the user can write the record between two steps.** In such a flow the nodes after a decision run as the decider (`packages/spec/src/automation/flow.zod.ts#runAs`), and once the decision is a closed outcome this lock refuses their writes to the record and its lines. Between two steps that refusal would end the run with the first step's outcome on the record and the second step never asked. So in a flow that runs as the user, no node that can write the record or a line of it stands between two steps. The author declares such a flow to run as system, or moves the write.
- **No flow of the approval pauses between two steps.** A `wait` or a `screen` there holds the record on the earlier step's outcome, closed, for as long as the pause lasts, with the next step not yet asked. A pause that waits on a person or on a signal can last for good, and a `screen` that shows an object's form leaves the save to the client, which is a caller's write (`packages/services/service-automation/src/builtin/screen-nodes.ts`). So no node that can pause the run of itself stands between two steps, in a flow that runs as the user or in one that runs as system. The check reads that off the node type's own declaration (`packages/spec/src/automation/node-executor.zod.ts#supportsPause`). A node whose type publishes no descriptor counts as one that can pause: the engine refuses a pause to a type whose descriptor declares none, and a type with no descriptor it lets pause (`packages/services/service-automation/src/run-resume.ts#refuseUndeclaredSuspension`). A `subflow` and a `map` are read by their bodies, and an approval of another record under a `map` is a pause as well, below. The revise window of a returned request is not such a pause: it follows `returned`, which the first condition keeps open.

Between two steps means after one step's closed outcome and before the next step opens. The next step can be the same step opened again: a `map` runs its body once for each item, and where the items are not records every one of those runs acts on the parent's record. A node after the last step of a `map` body therefore stands before that step's next opening, although the body's flow, read alone, shows it after its last step.

A `map` whose body holds an approval step is read both ways, for the record of the run that holds it, the parent's record. The check cannot read ahead whether an item is a record. Where an item is not one, the body's step acts on the parent's record: it counts as a step of that record, and names the field. Where an item is a record, the request is filed on the item, nothing lands on the parent's record while that request is open, and the parent's run waits on an approval of another record as it would wait on a `screen`: for the parent's record that is a pause. The body opens again for each item, and one collection can hold items of both kinds, so wherever such a `map` stands among the steps, that wait can fall between two steps of the parent's record. Where the conditions concern a step that can act on a record of the parent's object, the approval therefore holds no `map` with an approval step in its body, at any depth, unless the check can read from the node that none of its items is a record, as it can where the items are written out in the node. The steps of such a body are steps of the parent's record like any other. A `subflow` body runs on its parent's record, and its steps are that record's own.

Otherwise the node refuses the approval's first request: no request is written, nothing is mirrored, no lock is taken, and the error names the flow, the step, the object, the field and what is missing. With the conditions met, every request of the approval on a record of the object is mirrored onto the one field. Each request's `pending` lands there unless a rule or a hook of the app's own stops it, and the field is then on an open value from the request's opening until the request is decided. A record is on a closed value of its approval field in four situations only: the last approval that mirrored onto the field ended there; an approval is passing from one step's decision to the next step's opening, a passage in which no caller's write, no pause and no approval of another record stands; another writer put it there; or one of the cases the conditions do not reach, below.

What the conditions keep out is a record that reads a decision, and is closed, with no finished approval behind it. The mirror writes each request's outcome as it is decided, whether or not a later step follows. With no `pending` landing when the next step opens, the record stays on the earlier step's outcome while the later step is open, and for good where that step ends in a status the field does not declare, or is abandoned with nothing the mirror can put back. A later step that mirrors onto another field, or onto none, lands no `pending` on this one at all. A new approval of a decided document fails the same way: it leaves the old decision standing through a rejection the field cannot show. And a run that this lock stops between two steps, or that a pause or an approval of another record holds there, leaves the first step's outcome standing with no later step open.

An approval none of whose steps mirrors onto a field the object closes on is asked nothing. It moves no such field, so a record that is closed stays closed through it, on the value its last writer left, and a send-back in it opens no window on that record. Such an approval is not a way out of the closed state, and its author gates its start on the state.

**The approval is judged whole, once, at its first request.** That is the first point at which the objects and the flows are certainly all in hand: they are published apart, and any of them can change after the others. The node reads the approval from its definition: every approval step of the flow the record's run was started on, and of every flow that a `subflow` or a `map` in it names, to any depth. It judges each step on that step's own mirror field and on each object the step can act on: the object of the run's record, and under a `map` the object that node names for its items as well. Every step has therefore been judged before the first request is written, one that opens later on another record or another object too. A first step that names no mirror field does not open the approval unjudged: where another step that can act on the same object is one the conditions concern, the approval is refused for the step that names none. A later request of the same approval is not judged again: refusing it would stop the run on the earlier step's outcome, which is the outcome the check exists to keep out. A request that the plugin cannot place as first or later is not judged either, for the same reason, below. Where a part of the approval cannot be read ahead, a flow that a node names and the plugin cannot read, the approval cannot be judged, and its first request is refused. The same conditions are reported where a flow or an object is authored, wherever the parts are in view together; that report is early notice, and the refusal at the first request is the rule. The rule holds for an approval of one step as for one of several, since a single step fails too, on a decided document that is sent for approval again. In an application where no object declares the key, nothing is read and no approval is refused.

**What the conditions do not reach.** The platform reads ahead what can be read: a field's options, with the `visibleWhen` of those the mirror writes for a request's status, and its `readonlyWhen`; an object's closed values; the one move of a `state_machine` rule that a lock depends on; and the nodes of the approval's flows, with each step's mirror field, every node that writes or pauses, and every `map` that holds an approval step. An author who puts a `state_machine` rule on the mirror field owes the mirror its other moves as well, from one status to the next and out of `pending` and `returned` when a request is abandoned. A move the rule refuses leaves the field on the status it holds, which is then an open one: a stale status and no lock, as today. Four things cannot be read ahead, and each can leave a record on an earlier step's outcome, closed, with its approval unfinished. For the first three this record fixes what must hold and leaves the means to a card of its own ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 8), and the key is not released before that card is built.

- **A rule or a hook of the app's own that refuses a write of the mirror.** A rule of another kind than `state_machine`, or a hook, can turn on the record's data, so no reading of a definition shows what it will do. Where it refuses the `pending` of a later step, the record stays on the earlier step's outcome while that step is open. A `visibleWhen` on an option that is not a request's status can refuse a value the mirror puts back, and it is held to the same. What must hold: a write of the mirror that would take a record off a closed value its own approval wrote, and that does not land, is not swallowed, and the record is not left closed on that value.
- **A run that ends between two steps**: a node fails, or the run is cancelled. No reading of a definition shows every failure. The next step itself can fail to open, by the policy its own configuration declares for an empty slate, or because another request is pending on the record (`packages/spec/src/automation/approval.zod.ts#onEmptyApprovers`, `packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#openNodeRequest`). And a node can still fail on this lock, through a write the check cannot read: a hook of the app that writes back to the record, or a delete on another object whose cascade reaches it. No request is open then, so none of the three writers that abandon a request acts, and the sweep of dead runs reads pending requests only (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#releaseDeadRunRequests`). The record stays on the first step's outcome with the later steps never asked, the same kind of outcome as an abandoned request leaves. On `main` the same run leaves the same status on the record; what this record adds is that the status may now be a closed one. What must hold: such an end does not leave the record closed on a value its own approval wrote. An end after the last step is another case: there the approval is finished and its decision stands.
- **A decision that is recorded while its run cannot be resumed**, so that the run never goes on from it and the next step is never asked. `main` has a reader of the requests whose decision is recorded and whose run did not go on, and it reports them and changes nothing (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#inspectStrandedRequests`). The record is left as a run that ends between two steps leaves it, and the same must hold.
- **A change made after the approval opened**: an object that declares its closed values, or a `readonlyWhen` on the field, while the approval is in flight, or a flow that is changed while it runs. An approval in flight is not judged again. An approval whose run was started before the key was released is in the same case where the plugin cannot place its request, below.

A record that the fourth leaves closed has the ways out this record names: a new approval, or an action of the app's own.

What follows depends on how a request ends. Take the common case, a record that was closed on `approved`.

- **While the request is pending**, the pending lock holds the record where the approval node locks it, which is a node's default (`packages/spec/src/automation/approval.zod.ts#lockRecord`). Under a node that declares `lockRecord: false` nothing holds the record, and once the mirror has opened it a caller edits it as any open record.
- **Approved again**, the field holds `approved` and the record is closed again. Where another approval step follows, its request opens and the field holds `pending` once more.
- **Rejected**, the field holds `rejected` where it declares that value. Unless the object closes on that value too, the record is open and a caller edits it as any open record.
- **Returned**, the field holds `returned` where it declares that value. The request waits in its revise window, where the pending lock lets go of the record so that its submitter can revise it ([ADR-0044](./0044-approval-send-back-for-revision.md)).
- **Abandoned.** Three writers abandon a request: its submitter's recall, while the request is pending or after it was returned; an administrator's override, on a pending request; and the sweep that releases a record held by a dead run (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#recall`, `packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#abandonForDeadRun`). Each records `recalled`. On a field that declares `recalled`, the field then holds `recalled`. On a field that does not, the mirror on `main` puts back the value the field held when the request was submitted, or failing that the field's declared default, or empty. The rule below limits what the mirror may write there.

**An abandoned approval never writes a closed value.** Ruled 2026-11-26 (ruling record [`7201405662`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7201405662)). An approval that opened a record may have let it be edited: in the revise window of a returned request, or for as long as it ran, under a node that does not lock. Closed again over such a record, the state would stand as a decision on edits no approval has seen. The rule holds for all three writers that abandon a request. The seat reads it by the text of the option the maintainer chose, 「ADR 加一条：撤回永远不把状态写回关闭值」, the ADR adds a rule: a recall never writes the state back to a closed value ([`7202242844`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7202242844)). So no value the mirror writes for an abandoned request is a closed value of the mirror field, whatever that value says.

- **`recalled` is written where the field declares it and the object does not close on it**, as today. That value is the request's own status, and the record it leaves is open.
- **Where the object closes on `recalled`, the mirror does not write it.** The field is then treated as one that declares no `recalled` value, below. The reason is the rule's own: a record with edits no approval has seen is not closed again, and that holds whether the field would read `approved` or `recalled`. So listing `recalled` among a field's closed values closes nothing through the mirror. An app that wants a withdrawn document closed closes it with its own action or flow. This is the seat's reading of the rule, and the narrower one is in [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered).
- **A value the object declares closed on the mirror field is never put back.** Where there is no `recalled` value the mirror may write, because the field declares none or the object closes on it, the mirror looks for a value in this order: the value held at submit, then the field's declared default, then empty. It is the order the mirror uses today, with two departures. A declared closed value is passed over at each step. And on a required field an empty value at submit is passed over for the default, where today the mirror puts it back on every field: a required field that is not `readonly` refuses that write, and one that is `readonly` would be left empty. A record that was closed when it was submitted, and that the approval opened, is therefore left open with the value a record that was never submitted carries: the default, or empty. The mirror writes `approved` on it again only when it is approved again.
- **A closed value the approval did not write is left alone.** When a request is abandoned, the field holds `pending` or `returned` wherever the mirror's own writes have landed, and those are open values. Where it holds a closed value instead, the mirror asks whether the approval wrote it. For any request of the approval on this record the mirror may have written `pending`, `returned`, `approved` or `rejected`. Where the field holds a closed value that no request of the approval on this record had as one of those statuses, the mirror writes nothing: another writer put the record there, and anything the mirror wrote would open it. Where such a request did have it, the approval wrote it, and the mirror goes down the order, so that a draft goes back to its draft value. A request of the same approval on another record, an item of a `map`, does not count. An earlier step's outcome can still be on the field in the cases the conditions do not reach, above: a write of the mirror that did not land, or an object or a flow that changed after the approval opened. The mirror judges this by what the field holds, set against the approval's requests on the record. It does not ask the value at submit, which a request does not always carry. Where it cannot tell whether the approval wrote the value, it goes down the order: that costs a decision, and writing nothing could keep one that was never finished. It cannot tell in two cases, and both concern requests opened before requests recorded their root run: the abandoned request records none, or a request on the record that records none, opened since the approval's run started, had the value as its status. The rule assumes a mirror field that callers do not write, which is what the approval node's schema advises (`packages/spec/src/automation/approval.zod.ts#approvalStatusField`).

The last step can fail to land. An update may not empty a required field that is not `readonly`, so on such a field with no default the mirror can use, the write of empty is refused; the mirror logs a failed write and the field keeps the value it holds. The rule holds whether or not the write lands, since the mirror has written no closed value. Wherever the mirror's earlier writes landed, the value the field keeps is `pending` or `returned`, and an approval opens on no object that closes on either: the record carries a stale status and no lock, as it does today. [Execution](#execution-after-acceptance) has the approvals card run the case and pin it.

The rule is the approvals plugin's to keep, at the point where the mirror chooses its value. The engine does not judge this write, because a system context is not bound. The plugin reads the object's closed values from the registry it already reads the field's options from. A request's row carries the id of the run that opened it. A child run's context names its immediate parent and not the root of its tree, and the plugin's reader of a run does not answer a run's context (`packages/services/service-automation/src/builtin/subflow-node.ts`, `packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-node.ts#hasParentRun`). So the automation service hands every run of a tree its root run: that run's id, the flow it was started on, the object of its record and when it started. The approval node gives it to the plugin, and each request records its root run as it opens. A run that was started before this change carries no root run. The automation service derives one for it, from the run's links to its parents, when it resumes the run, so every request opened after the release records its root run, a request opened in a child run that was started before the release included. The plugin then asks its own table in one probe, with no walk of run links: when a request opens, whether its approval already has one; when a request is abandoned, whether a request of the approval on the record had the value the field holds. A request opened before requests recorded their root run records none, so the first probe can miss an approval's earlier requests. Where it finds none, the plugin asks whether a request that records no root run was opened since the approval's root run started. Where one was, the plugin cannot tell a first request from a later one, and it does not judge the request: a refusal there could fall between two steps. Only an approval whose run started before requests recorded their root run can be in that case, and that is before the key was released. An approval that is read whole and reaches no step the conditions concern is refused nothing. Its mirror writes as it does today only where the object closes on no value of the mirror field: the rule for an abandoned request holds wherever the object closes on the field, on a value of its own as on a status of the approval. The rule is released with the key ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 7).

This record keeps the path and names it: a new approval is a sanctioned way out of a state closed on the mirror field, and the app controls it at the flow's entry, by who may start the flow and from which states. An author who does not want a decided document sent for approval again gates the flow's start on the state, or closes on a business state the mirror does not write. An author who wants an abandoned approval to show on the document declares `recalled` among the field's options, and does not close on it. The other reading, that a request is not opened on a closed record, is in [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered), with the other things the mirror could have written. The maintainer kept the path in the first two rulings of 2026-11-26.

The mark is on the breadth of the exemption. The card leaves "system writes" to this record, and this record does not limit a system context to moving the state: it is not bound at all, as it is not bound by the pending lock. The narrower reading is in [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered).

Three reasons the system context passes here although `readonlyWhen` deliberately does not exempt it:

1. **There has to be a way out.** `readonlyWhen` can refuse system contexts because a hook-derived value inside the same write still lands, so the field keeps a server-side write path. A record-level lock with no exemption would have none: a paid invoice could never be refunded and an approval mirror could never move a decided document.
2. **One read cannot mean two things.** The pending lock passes a system context. The lock read of [D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer) answers for both producers with one word, `locked`, and a client must be able to read it without asking which rulebook applies.
3. **It is what the proof already does.** The showcase's hooks, written under the earlier ruling, pass a system session. Converting them to the declaration changes no line-level outcome for a system write.

What elevation does not reach today — identity assignment, append-only ledgers, the organization wall (item ⑤) — is unchanged and is not extended here. `readonlyWhen` remains the tool for a field that must hold even against system writes. An approval's mirror field is not such a field: the mirror has to write it.

### D6 — A closed record is not deleted by a caller

▲ A bound writer's delete of a closed record is refused `RECORD_LOCKED`.

This differs from the pending lock, where the record's delete lands and cancels the request. The difference is deliberate. A pending approval is a window with something to cancel, and the ruling behind it vetoed forbidding the delete. A closed state is where the document ends up; there is nothing to cancel, and deleting it is the largest change that can be made to it. Apps already hide the delete button on closed documents (the showcase does); under ADR-0124 the server enforces what that button promises.

- **Cascades.** A delete whose cascade would remove a closed record, or a line of a closed record, is refused whole. No row is removed by a delete this rule refuses.
  - **What is judged, and when.** The engine already collects the full set a delete will remove before it writes anything (`packages/objectql/src/engine-cascade-planning.ts#cascadeDeleteRelations`). The closed-state judgement is made on that whole set, at the point the engine's own in-cascade `restrict` refusals are made: after the caller's gates on the record it asked to delete, and before the first row is removed. It is not made leg by leg behind each row's own gates. The legs are deleted one at a time in the caller's context, and on a store with no transactions, or where the cascade crosses two datasources, a refusal met at a later leg would leave the earlier rows gone.
  - **What the refusal names.** `RECORD_LOCKED`, in a sentence that names two things: the record the caller asked to delete, by its object's label and its title as the caller is served it ([D8](#d8--the-refusal)), and the object whose closed record stands in the way, by its label. It carries no title, id or count of the closed records. The engine's `restrict` refusal draws the same line for rows the caller may not read: it names the dependent object, and gives a count only where the caller's own read would return the same rows (`packages/objectql/src/engine-delete-cascade.ts#deleteRestrictedError`, `packages/objectql/src/engine-cascade-planning.ts#dependentCountIsDisclosable`). This refusal gives no count at all.
  - **An exception, in so many words.** A caller who may delete the record at the root learns that a closed record of the named object hangs below it, whether or not that caller may read that record. It is accepted. The other order would give up "refused whole", and a delete that removes part of what it was asked to remove is the worse outcome.
- **A system context deletes** ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)): retention, the removal of an organization, an app's own action.
- **"The record's own delete" as an exemption**, which the card asks about, therefore exists only where the delete itself is allowed: when a system context deletes a closed record, its lines go with it through the cascade and no leg is refused.

### D7 — Writes that are not a caller's change

Some writes reach a closed record or its lines without being anyone's edit. Each is named here so the build does not have to guess.

- **Referential clears land.** When a record is deleted, the engine clears optional lookups that pointed at it, in the caller's context. On a closed record, or a line of one, that clear lands: exactly the cleared field, at exactly the value the engine wrote, and nothing else in the same payload. This is the exemption `readonlyWhen` already gives the same write (`packages/objectql/src/validation/rule-validator.ts#ReferentialClearExemption`), including the recycle-bin restore's re-link of that reference.
- **Engine-derived writes that run as system pass by D5.** The build card for the engine lists each one it meets and pins it with a test; a derived write that turns out to run in the caller's context is reported back, not exempted on the spot.
- **Restore.** A recycle-bin entry comes back as it stood, closed or not: restore writes rows back, it does not edit them (ADR-0148 D2). One case is refused before anything is written: an entry that would put a row back under a **live, closed** master that is not itself part of the entry. That is adding a line to a closed document through another door, and it answers `RECORD_LOCKED`. A restore run in a system context passes.
- **A change of owner is not one of them.** Change Owner moves each record by an ordinary by-id update in the caller's context (`packages/core/src/security/change-record-owner.ts`), so it is bound like any edit. On a closed record, or a line of one, it answers `RECORD_LOCKED`. The bulk form moves the records it can and lists each closed one among its failures, by id, with that code and its sentence. Its cascade is made of the same updates: a child row that is closed, or is a line of a closed record, stays with its previous owner and is reported the same way, and a record that did not move cascades nothing. The hand-over of a banned user's work writes as the caller too (`packages/core/src/security/user-assigned-work.ts`): the closed records that user holds stay with that user and are counted among the failures. A closed document changes hands through the app's own action or a system context. An exemption for the owner field is left open ([Points the ruling does not settle](#points-the-ruling-does-not-settle)).

### D8 — The refusal

- **One code: `409 RECORD_LOCKED`.** It is the code clients already handle for the pending lock, and its meaning to a client is the same: this record is locked, and the write did not land. The engine package becomes an emitter of the code in the ADR-0112 ledger (`packages/spec/src/api/error-code-ledger.zod.ts`), beside the approvals plugin and the upload-link door.
- **Its own sentence.** The pending lock's end-user sentence tells the reader to wait until the approval completes (`packages/spec/src/system/operation-message.ts#record_locked`). That is false for a closed document, so the closed-state refusal has its own sentence family in the Operation Message Catalog, in every locale the catalog carries, with the same four forms (with object label and title, without a title, without a label, naming nothing).
- **What the sentence may name.** The closed document, by its object's label and its title. The title is the one the writer would be served: picked from the row under the writer's own field-level security, the way the pending lock picks its title (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/lifecycle-hooks.ts#recordTitleOf`, `#writerEnvelope`). A display field the writer may not be served gives no title, and the sentence takes the label form. ⛔ Never the title as stored. For a line write it names the master and takes the label-only form, as the pending line lock does: the engine reads the master's state to judge, and reads nothing of the master to word the refusal. The sentence never carries a record id, an API name, or the state's value: a reader who may see the state reads it off the record.
- **What the refusal tells, and to whom.** That the document is closed, to a caller whom the object's permission and the row's access let write it ([D10](#d10--where-it-is-enforced-and-how-it-sits-beside-the-mechanisms-that-exist)). The two exceptions, a line that hangs under a master its writer cannot read and a delete's cascade, are stated where they arise ([D10](#d10--where-it-is-enforced-and-how-it-sits-beside-the-mechanisms-that-exist), [D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)). Where an object declares one value on one field, "closed" is one bit of that field, and a writer who may change the document but may not read the field learns it. That is accepted: a refused writer is owed the true reason, and the pending lock's "wait for the approval" would be false. The lock read tells every reader of the record the same fact ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)).
- **The upload-link door words its own refusal.** It answers `RECORD_LOCKED` in a sentence of its own, in every locale it carries, and that sentence says an approval is in progress (`packages/services/service-storage/src/upload-link-copy.ts`). It gains a closed-state sentence beside it, and chooses between the two by the lock read's reason ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)).
- **A cascade's refusal has its own sentence.** A delete refused for what its cascade would reach says that the record the caller asked to delete cannot be deleted because a closed record of the named object stands in the way. The wording covers both cases [D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller) judges: the closed record would be removed with it, or a line of the closed record would be removed while the closed record stays. The sentence names the two things [D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller) allows and nothing else, in every locale the catalog carries. It takes the label form, with no title, where the caller is served no title for the record at the root, and where the delete names many records.
- **The refusal does not say which lock.** Both locks answer the same code, and nothing machine-readable in the refusal names the producer. A client that must tell them apart asks the lock read. Whether the refusal's body should carry the reason is left open ([Points the ruling does not settle](#points-the-ruling-does-not-settle)).
- **When both locks hold**, either refusal is correct and both carry the same code. The closed-state judgement runs before the before-phase hooks ([D10](#d10--where-it-is-enforced-and-how-it-sits-beside-the-mechanisms-that-exist)), so the closed sentence is the one the writer sees.

### D9 — One lock read, owned by the data layer

▲ There is one read that answers "is this record locked" for both producers. It is owned by the data layer, and every first-party door that asks reads it.

```ts
// For one record, as the caller. `null`: the caller cannot read the record, or it does not exist.
// `lockSource` and `lockReason` are present exactly when `locked` is true.
{ locked: boolean; lockSource?: 'record' | 'master'; lockReason?: 'closed_state' | 'approval' } | null
```

- **Two producers, both named here.** The engine's own closed-state judgement, and the approvals plugin's pending-request judgement (the one behind today's `getRecordLock`). The read composes them. A third producer is a new decision, not a registration: the seam between the engine and the approvals plugin is internal.
- **It exists without the approvals plugin.** An app that installs no approvals still declares closed states and still has a lock read.
- **`locked` means locked for a caller** ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)). The read does not answer for system contexts.
- **`lockReason` says which producer holds.** A client words its banner from it: "in approval" and "closed" are different things to tell a user. It is the one member this read adds to the answer the approvals contract publishes.
- **Every reader of the record is told `locked`, and the reason with it.** The read answers a caller who can read the record, whether or not that caller may write it, and whether or not field permissions let it read the state field. This is the line the maintainer drew for the pending lock on 2026-10-26 ([#25934](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/25934), option A): the lock fact goes to every caller who can read the record, so that no client has to guess and none offers an edit the server will refuse. The approvals contract carries that promise today (`packages/spec/src/contracts/approval-service.ts#getRecordLock`).
- **What that discloses, and why it is accepted.** `closed_state` is a statement about a field. To a reader whom field permissions keep from the state field it gives one bit of that field: the record is in one of its declared closed states. For a line the reader is a reader of the line, and it is told that the line's master is closed whether or not it may read the master, as the approvals read tells a line's reader today that its master is held. The read does not say which field or which value, and of a master it says nothing but `lockSource: 'master'`. The 2026-10-26 ruling disclosed that an approval is in progress, which is not a field's value. This is the first place the platform lets one bit of a field out to a reader the field is hidden from, so the maintainer was asked, and accepted it on 2026-11-26. Three reasons carried it. It runs the way the 2026-10-26 ruling runs. Withholding it would protect only a reader who can neither write the record nor read the field: a reader who can write is told by the refusal itself ([D8](#d8--the-refusal)), after being offered an edit that fails. And it is one rule where the other reading needs two. An author for whom a field's state is itself confidential to some readers does not declare `closedStates` on that field ([Consequences](#consequences)).
- **`lockSource` says whose lock it is, and its delete reading belongs to approvals.** `'record'`: the record's own state, or its own request. `'master'`: its master's. The approvals contract also reads a delete rule off this member: a record held on its own "may still be deleted", a line of a held master may not (`packages/spec/src/contracts/approval-service.ts#getRecordLock`), and the console's grid keeps or drops Delete by it (`ui/packages/plugin-grid/src/objectGridActionsColumn.tsx`). On this read that rule holds **only under `lockReason: 'approval'`**. Under `closed_state` a caller's delete is refused for either source ([D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)). A client reads the reason first; a locked answer always carries one.
- **One answer when several hold.** The reason is chosen first, `closed_state` before `approval`; within a reason, `master` before `record`, which is today's rule. A closed document with a stale pending request reads as closed.
- **What it never carries.** Which field or value closed the record, a request id, or participants. The same disclosure line the read has today.
- **Readable means what it means today, door by door.** The read is made as the caller; a record the caller cannot read answers `null`, the same as a missing one. The approvals contract keeps two readability anchors on purpose, one for the single read and one for the page read (`packages/spec/src/contracts/approval-service.ts#getRecordLocks` records the difference and the seat ruling behind it). Each form of this read keeps the anchor of its counterpart, and this record aligns neither. The page form keeps today's bound of 200 ids (`#APPROVAL_RECORD_LOCKS_MAX_RECORD_IDS`).
- **Doors.** The data family gains the read for one record and for a page of records, with SDK methods beside the other data reads. The exact path spelling is the build card's.
- **The approvals-family read stays exactly as published.** Its two routes, their SDK methods and `IApprovalService.getRecordLock` / `getRecordLocks` go on answering the question their contract words, whether a record is held by a locking approval, with `lockSource` and its delete reading as published. They do not answer for closed states. Serving them from the new read would widen a published answer, and would hand a closed record's `lockSource: 'record'` to clients that read it as "may still be deleted". The seat ruling that contract records (7152650156) declined to narrow a published answer on this read; widening one is the same kind of change, and it is not made here in passing. A client that asks only the approvals read is told nothing about closed states, and the server's refusal is its answer (ADR-0124). Retiring those routes, or redirecting them, is decided on a card of its own, filed with the build cards ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 6).
- **First-party askers move to the one read.** The console's record page, grid and line grid, and the upload-link door that asks before it issues a link. The record page's approvals part keeps its `lock` as the approvals producer's answer (`packages/rest/src/record-page-routes.ts#answerApprovals`).

The pending lock's write side is untouched. One read for both producers, two enforcers: the engine for closed states, the approvals plugin's hooks for pending requests.

### D10 — Where it is enforced, and how it sits beside the mechanisms that exist

- **In the engine's write path.** Not in an optional plugin and not in a hook an app could forget: a declared `closedStates` is enforced wherever a record of the object can be written (ADR-0049, ADR-0078). The key and its enforcement ship in the same release, and the showcase conversion is the runtime proof ADR-0054 asks for.
- **After the gates that say who may write.** For the record's own write and for a line write, the judgement is made once two gates have answered: the object's permission and the row's access. A caller either of them refuses gets exactly the answer an open record gives, and a predicate write is judged only over the rows the caller's own scope reaches. Field permissions on what a write carries are asked earlier still: the security layer judges them in its own middleware, ahead of the engine's hooks, and refuses the write there (`packages/plugins/plugin-security/src/security-plugin-authz-middleware.ts`).
- **A line write reaches a master in one of two ways, and each has its rule.** A write may *name* a master, or concern the master its stored row *already hangs under*. A move does both, once for each end.
  - **A master the write names: the caller's read door first.** A create of a line names its master, and so does an update that puts a line under a master its stored row does not point at. A caller may name as master only a row its own read door returns. A write that names any other row answers `reference_not_found`, the same as for a row that exists nowhere, and on `main` that gate runs after the before-phase hooks (`packages/objectql/src/engine-referential-integrity.ts#assertReferencesResolve`). The line judgement does not run ahead of that question. The engine asks the caller's read door the way that gate asks it (`packages/objectql/src/engine-references-and-master-detail.ts#callerReadableReferenceExists`) and consults the named master's state only for a row the door returned. For any other row the lock does not answer, and the write goes on to the reference gate's own refusal. This is the order in which `readonlyWhen` already reads a header: may the caller see it, then what it holds (`packages/objectql/src/engine-references-and-master-detail.ts#resolveMasterDetailParent`).
  - **A master the line already hangs under: its stored state, whoever the writer is.** An update of a line in place, a delete of a line, and the leaving end of a move concern the master the stored row points at. The reference gate does not ask again about a value a row already stores, and a delete does not pass that gate, so nothing behind the lock would refuse these writes. They are judged on that master's stored state. The engine reads it itself, above the writer's grants and inside the writer's organization scope, whether or not the writer may read the master. The pending line lock judges the same writes the same way. `readonlyWhen` does not: a header the caller's read door does not return leaves `parent` unbound there, and a field whose predicate needs it holds as locked whatever the header's state (`packages/objectql/src/engine-references-and-master-detail.ts#resolveMasterDetailParent`). That stays the rule for `readonlyWhen`. It is not this lock's rule, for the reason given in [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered).
- **Gates asked of the row a write would produce stay where they are.** The check on the written image runs after the before-phase hooks on `main` (`packages/objectql/src/engine-top-level.ts#postHookWriteImageCheck`). So does validation, and so does the reference gate for every reference but the master a line write names. The judgement does not wait for them. A caller only such a gate would refuse is still a caller who may write the stored record, and is told it is closed.
- **What this delivers, and where it stops.** The promise is this wide and no wider. For the record's own write and for a line write, a closed record answers no differently from an open one to a caller whom the object's permission or the row's access refuses, and a closed master answers no differently from an open one to a caller who names it and may not read it. It is a statement about those gates. It says nothing of the gates that run later, on the row a write would produce. Two cases fall outside it and are accepted in so many words. A caller who may write a line that already hangs under a master is told that the master is closed, whether or not that caller may read the master. That is one bit of the master's state, given to a writer the refusal is owed to ([D8](#d8--the-refusal)); the pending line lock tells the same writer that the master is in approval. And a delete's cascade is judged as [D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller) says.
- **Before the before-phase hooks.** A write refused for its own record, or for its own master, runs no app hook. A delete refused for what its cascade would reach is refused where the cascade is planned, before any row is removed ([D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)).
- **Free when not declared.** Whether an object declares the key, or is a line of one that does, is read from the registry. An object that is neither pays nothing.

| Mechanism | After this record |
|---|---|
| Pending-approval lock | Unchanged. Both locks may hold the same record; either refuses. |
| Approval status mirror | Two changes, both only where an object closes on the mirror field. An approval whose step mirrors onto a field the object closes on at `pending`, `returned`, `approved` or `rejected` opens only where the conditions of [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not) hold: `pending` declared, neither `pending` nor `returned` closed, the move to `pending` that a `state_machine` rule owes a closed `approved` or `rejected`, no `readonlyWhen` on the field and no `visibleWhen` on its option for a request's status, one mirror field for every step that can act on the object, and between two steps no write to the record by a flow that runs as the user and no pause, on a node or on an approval of another record under a `map`. Otherwise its first request is refused. And where a request is abandoned, on whichever values the object closes, the mirror never writes a closed value, leaves alone a closed value the approval did not write, and passes over an empty value at submit on a required field. What becomes of a write of the mirror that does not land, and of an approval whose run stops between two steps, is a card of its own that the key waits for ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 8). Every other write of the mirror is unchanged. |
| `readonlyWhen` | Unchanged, with its own rules (item ③). Still the tool for "these fields freeze in these states", and for a field that must hold against system writes. On a caller's write to a closed record the closed-state refusal answers first, so nothing is stripped. One field takes none: the mirror field of an approval on an object that closes on its outcome, which the mirror has to write ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)). |
| `state_machine` | Unchanged. It says which moves are legal for whoever may make them. `closedStates` says when a caller may write at all. The approval node reads the rule when an approval opens ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)). |
| `userActions` predicates | Unchanged, and still display only. The console reads the lock read to grey edit and hide delete on a closed record, so an author no longer has to spell that out per object. |
| Permissions | Unchanged. A closed state grants nothing and no grant lifts it. |

## Execution (after acceptance)

Nothing below starts before this record is Accepted. The seat files the cards; this list is their scope, not their text. Each is a child of #36253.

1. **The key and its enforcement, released together.** The spec key with its authoring refusals, form row, reference docs and liveness proof ([D1](#d1--the-key-closedstates)); the engine's judgement on update, delete, line writes, many-row writes through the store's probe, the cascade set and restore, placed behind the permission and row-access gates ([D2](#d2--closed-is-read-off-the-stored-row-nothing-is-stored-for-it) to [D7](#d7--writes-that-are-not-a-callers-change), [D10](#d10--where-it-is-enforced-and-how-it-sits-beside-the-mechanisms-that-exist)); the ledger row, the sentence family and the cascade's sentence in every locale, and the upload-link door's own closed-state sentence in every locale that door carries ([D8](#d8--the-refusal)). One PR, or a trunk branch where the work spans cards (`AGENTS.md`: a stacked series is not a supported form), but the key is never published without its enforcement (ADR-0078). `Metadata-protocol: added`.
2. **The one read.** The engine's read, the internal seam the approvals producer answers through, the data-family routes and SDK methods, and the first-party askers moved ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)). The approvals-family routes and contract members are not touched.
3. **The showcase conversion — the proof.** The three paid-invoice line hooks are deleted and the invoice declares `closedStates`. The card records, as a dogfood run against the running app, each refusal the hooks gave before and the declaration gives after, and every outcome that changes by design:
   - a caller's edit of a paid invoice's header is refused, where today every field but the one that carries `readonlyWhen` lands;
   - a caller's delete of a paid invoice is refused, where today only the button is hidden;
   - a caller's update of a line under a paid invoice is refused whole, where today a change to `description`, `position`, `service_start` or `receipt` lands, and a write that carries `product`, `quantity` or `unit_price` has those keys stripped and the rest lands;
   - adding, moving and removing a line under a paid invoice stay refused, now with the platform's code and sentence where the app worded three of its own.
4. **The console.** The record page, the grid and the line grid read the one read and word the reason; edit is greyed on a locked record and delete hidden where the read says `closed_state`, without per-object predicates; the master-detail save writes a closing header last ([D4](#d4--what-a-closed-unit-refuses-and-when-it-is-judged)).
5. **Docs.** The approvals guide's "the record unlocks" passages gain a pointer to closed states; the data-modelling guide documents the key, with the authoring notes of [Consequences](#consequences), every one of them. The recommendations the maintainer adopted on 2026-11-26 ask for three by name: what an action button does on a closed document, what the lock read tells a reader who may not see the state field, and a new approval of a closed document, with what an abandoned approval leaves behind.
6. **The approvals-family lock read.** A card filed with the build cards decides whether its two routes retire or are redirected to the one read ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)). Until that card is ruled they stay as published.
7. **The approvals plugin: the check when an approval opens, and the status mirror on an abandoned request.** Both are [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)'s, and both are released with item 1: the key is not published while an approval can open on a field that cannot show it, or an abandoned approval can still write a closed value.
   - **The root run.** The automation service hands every run of a tree, to any depth, its root run: that run's id, the flow it was started on, the object of its record and when it started. The approval node passes it to the plugin, and each request records its root run as it opens, in a column of its own, so that the store can be asked for it: the two stamps a request carries today live in its config snapshot, which the store cannot filter on. A run that was started before this change gets its root run when the service resumes it, derived from the run's links to its parents, so that every request opened after the release records one. This part changes the automation service as well as the plugin. Its test: a child run that was started before the release and opens a request after it, whose request records its root run.
   - **The check.** At an approval's first request the node reads every approval step of the root run's flow and of each flow that a `subflow` or a `map` in it names, to any depth, with each step's own mirror field and each object the step can act on, a `map`'s item object included. Where the object closes on a step's field at `pending`, `returned`, `approved` or `rejected`, it refuses the request unless all of this holds: the field declares `pending`; the object closes on neither `pending` nor `returned`; a `state_machine` rule on the field that declares the moves out of a closed `approved` or `rejected` has `pending` among them; the field carries no `readonlyWhen`, and none of its options for a request's status carries a `visibleWhen`; every approval step that can act on a record of the object names that field, a step in a `map` body counting as one that can act on the parent's record; no flow of the approval that runs as the user holds a node that can write the record or a line of it between two steps; no flow of the approval holds there a node that can pause the run of itself, a node whose type declares nothing counting as one that can; and the approval holds no `map` with an approval step in its body, at any depth, unless the node shows that none of its items is a record. Between two steps is after one step's closed outcome and before the next step opens, and the next step can be the same step opened again, so a node after the last step of a `map` body stands between two steps. The check reads a record node by the object the node names, a script function by the effect it declares, and a pause by what the node's type declares. A node whose writes it cannot read counts as one that writes, and a node it cannot place counts as standing between two steps. An approval it cannot read whole is refused. A request it cannot place as first or later, because a request that records no root run was opened since the approval's root run started, is not judged. The refusal names what is missing. The same conditions are reported at authoring wherever the parts are in view together. Its tests cover each missing condition; an object that closes on the field only on values of its own, which is never refused, also where its `state_machine` rule keeps such a value final; an application with no declaration, in which nothing is read; an approval none of whose steps names a closing field, which is not refused; a step that names no mirror field, and one that names another field, ahead of a step the conditions concern and after one, each of which is refused; a `map` whose body holds an approval step, which is refused where the check cannot read its items, ahead of the parent's steps, between two of them and after the last, also where the node names an object for its items; the same `map` with its items written out in the node, read as steps of the parent's record where none is a record and refused where one is; and the same `map` in an approval that concerns no step of the parent's object, which is not refused; a step two levels down, and two child runs side by side; a flow the node cannot read; a writing node between two steps in a flow that runs as the user, in one that runs as system, and in a child flow of each kind; a writing node after the only step of a `map` body; a `wait` and a `screen` between two steps, in a flow of each kind, a node there whose type publishes no descriptor, and the revise window, which is not refused; an option for each status of a request that carries a `visibleWhen`; a later request of an approval that opened before the object declared its closed values, which is not refused; and a later request of an approval whose earlier requests record no root run, which is not judged.
   - **The mirror.** On every transition that abandons a request it reads the object's closed values for the mirror field, whichever they are, never writes one, `recalled` included, and leaves alone a closed value the approval did not write. It places the abandoned request by its root run and asks its own table, in one probe, whether a request of the same approval on the same record had the value the field holds. Its tests cover the three writers that abandon a request, and across them: a field that declares `recalled` as an open value, as a closed value and not at all; a value at submit that is closed, open, empty on a required field, empty on a field that is not required, where it is still put back, and not carried by the request; a field that holds an open value, the request's own status or another, with and without a value at submit; a field that holds a closed value the approval did not write; an object that closes on the field only on values of its own, where no such value is put back and one the approval did not write is left alone; a field that holds an earlier step's outcome, with that step in the same run, in a child run, and in the parent of the run whose request is abandoned; a `map` in which only another item's request had the value the field holds, which is left alone; an abandoned request that records no root run, and an earlier request on the record that records none and was opened since the approval's run started, both of which go down the order; and a default that is usable, closed or absent.
   - **The docs.** The approvals guide says what an abandoned approval leaves, what a field and a flow owe an approval on an object that closes on its outcome, and what a refused opening is for the person who submitted. Among what a flow owes it names the one mirror field for every step and the pause that may not stand between two steps. It says that an approval of each item under a `map` runs as an approval of its own, and that an approval which does not mirror onto the closing field does not open a closed document. It keeps the word round for the resubmissions of [ADR-0044](./0044-approval-send-back-for-revision.md).
8. **The status mirror: a write that does not land, and a run that stops between two steps.** A card filed with the build cards: the child card the recommendations adopted on 2026-11-26 give the mirror's failed write ([`7202242844`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7202242844)). [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not) fixes what it delivers and leaves it the means. On a field the object closes on, none of three things leaves a record closed on a value its own approval wrote and did not finish: a write of the mirror that a rule or a hook of the app refuses, or a `visibleWhen` on an option the mirror puts back, which is also no longer swallowed; a run that ends between two steps because a node fails or the run is cancelled; and a decision that is recorded while its run cannot be resumed, so that the next step is never asked. The card settles how a refused write is shown, how the plugin learns that an approval's run has ended or will not go on, how it tells a stop between two steps from one after the last step, where the decision stands, and what the platform then does with the record. The reader of stranded requests on `main` is a starting point for the second question (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#inspectStrandedRequests`). Its first candidate for the refused write is to set the object's rules aside for that one write ([Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered)). Item 1 waits for this card: the key is not released before this card is built. Its tests pin the three cases, with the value the field keeps in each. For a run that ends between two steps they include a next step that fails to open, on an empty slate and on a request already pending on the record, and a node that fails on this lock through a write the check cannot read: a hook that writes back to the record, and a delete on another object whose cascade reaches it.

Four things the build verifies and pins, because this record read them but did not run them:

- The approval status mirror is written after the request is finalised, and a failure of that write is logged and swallowed (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#mirrorStatusField`). Where the mirrored value is a declared closed state, the document is open between the two writes, and stays open if the mirror fails. The engine card measures whether both writes share one unit of work and reports what it finds; what the plugin does about a write of the mirror that does not land is the card of item 8.
- Which engine-derived writes reach a closed record, and in which context each runs ([D7](#d7--writes-that-are-not-a-callers-change)).
- Whether a customization overlay may add `closedStates` to an installed package's object, and that it may not shorten a package's own list, under the overlay rules in force when the spec card is built. If the answer changes what a subscriber can do to a packaged document, it comes back as a decision card.
- What the mirror does when its put-back comes down to empty on a required field. The record validator refuses an update that empties a required field that is not `readonly` (`packages/objectql/src/validation/record-validator.ts`), and the mirror logs and swallows a failed write, so the field should keep the value it holds ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)). On a `readonly` field the validator judges the shape of the value only, so the write of empty should land. The approvals card runs both and pins them, with the value the field keeps in each.

## Points the ruling does not settle

The ruling says the object declares the states and that the record and its lines are closed to change. Four decisions above choose among readings it leaves open. The maintainer ruled on all four on 2026-11-26 (ruling record [`7200732942`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7200732942)) and adopted each as written. The table keeps the other reading beside each, as it stood before the ruling.

| ▲ | Decision | What it means for a user | The other reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not): a system context is not bound at all, where the card's words require only a sanctioned action that moves the state | A user, an administrator included, cannot edit or reopen a closed document by hand. The app's own actions and system automation can, and they can also change a closed document without reopening it: an action button's script works on a closed document as on an open one. A new approval of a document closed on its approval field moves it on as well. | Elevated automation may move only the state, and must reopen a document before changing it. |
| 2 | [D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller): a caller cannot delete a closed record | Delete is refused on a closed document, where a pending one can still be deleted. | Delete lands with its lines, as under the pending lock. |
| 3 | [D4](#d4--what-a-closed-unit-refuses-and-when-it-is-judged): judged on the stored row only. **Ruled 2026-11-26: item ③ governs `readonlyWhen`, and this lock is judged on the stored row.** | A user can close a document by setting its state, and "edit and mark paid" in one save works; an edit after that does not. Inside one save, the closing write goes last. | Item ③ reaches this lock too: it also holds when the write's result is closed. A user can then neither leave nor enter a closed state by an edit. Only an action, system automation or the approval mirror closes a document, and an app that closes by hand ships an action first. |
| 4 | [D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer): a new read owned by the data layer answers for both producers and names the reason to every reader of the record; the approvals-family read stays as published and answers for approvals only | The console can tell "in approval" from "closed", with or without the approvals plugin. A client that still asks the approvals read is not told about closed states, and meets the server's refusal. | Serve the approvals routes from the new read and accept that their published answer widens. Or keep the read in the approvals plugin and add a second read for closed states. |

The same ruling settled three points the contract review of this record had left open:

- **Who is told the lock's reason**: every caller who can read the record ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)). The other reading, that the read says nothing of closed states to a reader who may not read the state field, is in [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered).
- **How a delete's cascade is judged, and what its refusal names**: whole, before the first row is removed, naming the record at the root and the label of the object in the way ([D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)).
- **Whether a new approval stays a way out of a closed state**: it stays. [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not) names each outcome.

A second ruling the same day (ruling record [`7201405662`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7201405662)) settled what the third contract review raised on that path: an abandoned approval never writes a closed value ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)). Four things inside that ruling are this record's own. One is a reading: a `recalled` that the object closes on is not written either. The maintainer's own word is 恢复, restore, and the seat reads the rule by the text of the option the maintainer chose, which says a recall never writes the state back to a closed value ([`7202242844`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7202242844)). Three are choices: what the mirror writes in place of a closed value; that it leaves alone a closed value the approval did not write, judged by what the field holds against the requests of the whole approval on the record, child runs included, and goes down the order where it cannot tell; and that on a required field an empty value at submit is passed over for the default. The reading and the choices not taken are in [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered).

A third ruling the same day (ruling record [`7205949718`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7205949718)) settled the fifth marked point: whether a document may be left reading a decision, and closed, when its approval was abandoned before it finished ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)). The seventh contract review raised it, and the maintainer chose 「平台拒绝这种配置」: the platform refuses the configuration that allows it. The option the maintainer chose names two things a field may lack, an in-progress status and a way back, and two moments for the error, publishing and the opening of an approval. Inside that ruling fourteen things are this record's own or the seat's, and none of them is in the ruling's words:

- the refusal is made when an approval opens and reported at authoring, and is not made at publish, where the option's words allow either;
- it holds for an approval of one step as for one of several;
- an object may close on neither `pending` nor `returned` where an approval mirrors onto the field;
- one move of a `state_machine` rule is asked for, the move to `pending`;
- the conditions reach only an object that closes on a status the mirror writes, and the move is asked only of a closed `approved` or `rejected`, so an object that closes on values of its own is asked nothing (the seat's ruling on the eighth contract review);
- the approval is judged whole at its first request, from its definition, each step on its own field and its own object, and an approval that cannot be read whole is refused (the same ruling);
- a mirror field carries no `readonlyWhen` at all, where the seat's ruling asks only that none can hold on a closed value;
- a flow that runs as the user and can write the record between two steps is refused, where the seat's ruling left this record to choose between that and letting the approval's own run pass the lock;
- a run that ends between two steps is held to the same bound as a write of the mirror that does not land, and the key waits for the card that delivers both (the seat's ruling);
- every approval step that can act on a record of the object mirrors onto the one field. The seat's ruling on the ninth contract review asks it of a step that can follow one the conditions concern. Two readings inside it are this record's own: the first step of a new approval of a decided document follows the last decision, so the steps ahead are asked too; and a step in a `map` body counts as one that can act on the parent's record;
- no option of the field for a request's status carries a `visibleWhen`, which the check refuses as it refuses a `readonlyWhen` (the same ruling);
- a pause between two steps is refused when the approval opens, where the seat's ruling left this record to choose between that and holding a run parked there to the bound of item 8. A decision whose run cannot be resumed cannot be read ahead, and goes to that card (the same ruling). A node whose type declares nothing counts as one that can pause (the seat's ruling on the tenth contract review);
- a `map` whose body holds an approval step is read both ways, as a step of the parent's record and as a pause for it, and is refused unless the node shows that none of its items is a record (the same ruling). That it is refused wherever it stands among the steps follows from a collection that can hold items of both kinds;
- a request the plugin cannot place as first or later is not judged, so that no approval is refused between two steps (the same ruling).

What the refusal does not reach is stated in [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not), all of it: a rule or a hook of the app that refuses a write of the mirror, a run that ends between two steps, and a decision that is recorded while its run cannot be resumed, all three of which go to a card the key waits for ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 8); and a change made after the approval opened, which is not judged again. The other answer, to accept the outcome with a note to authors, is in [Alternatives considered](#alternatives-considered) with the ways not taken.

Left open on purpose, each a later decision if it is ever wanted:

- **Ownership transfer of a closed document.** A caller's transfer is refused, record by record, and a system context transfers ([D7](#d7--writes-that-are-not-a-callers-change)). If day-to-day reassignment of closed documents, or handing on the closed documents of a user who has left, proves necessary, exempting the ownership field is an amendment to D5.
- **Fields that stay editable on a closed record** (a note, a tag). Not offered. An app that needs it keeps the field on a satellite object.
- **Deeper levels of lines.** One level, as the pending lock ([D3](#d3--the-unit-the-record-and-its-direct-master_detail-lines)).
- **State fields that are not a `select`** (a boolean "posted"). Not admitted by [D1](#d1--the-key-closedstates).
- **Retiring the approvals-family lock routes, or redirecting them to the one read.** A card of its own, filed with the build cards ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 6).
- **A reason in the refusal's body.** The refusal carries one code for both locks ([D8](#d8--the-refusal)). If a client ever needs the producer from the refusal itself, the member carries what `lockReason` carries on the read.

## Consequences

**What gets better**

- "This document is closed" is one line on the object. New fields and new line objects are covered without anyone remembering them.
- The server enforces it at every door. A hidden button and a refused write now say the same thing.
- A client asks one question, gets one answer, and can word it.
- The showcase loses three hooks and gains a declaration that any app can copy.

**What an author has to know**

- **Publishing a declaration closes existing records at once** ([D2](#d2--closed-is-read-off-the-stored-row-nothing-is-stored-for-it)). There is no grace period and no backfill to wait for.
- **Automation that acts as the caller is refused on a closed record.** A flow that runs as the user and stamps a field after the document closes fails with `RECORD_LOCKED`. Either the stamp belongs in the write that closes the document, where a before-phase hook can derive it, or the flow is declared to run as system. The same holds for a hook that writes back to the record after it closed.
- **That includes the steps after an approval decision.** Where the approval's outcome is a declared closed state, the document is closed the moment the decision is mirrored, and the flow's next node meets a closed record. The pending lock let the run that opened the request write on; this lock does not. An author who closes on the approval's outcome declares that flow to run as system, or closes on a later state that the flow's own last node sets. Where such a write stands between two approval steps, the platform refuses the approval when it opens ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)).
- **A new approval reopens a document that is closed on its approval field, and an abandoned approval does not close it again.** An object that closes on its approval's own outcome, `approved` or `rejected`, owes the approval six things. The field declares `pending`, and the object closes on neither `pending` nor `returned`. Where a `state_machine` rule governs the field, a closed `approved` or `rejected` may move to `pending`. The field carries no `readonlyWhen`, and its options for a request's statuses carry no `visibleWhen`; `readonly` is what keeps callers off it. Every approval step of the flow, and of the flows it calls, that can act on the object names that one field: a step with another status field, or with none, is refused wherever it stands. A `map` whose body holds an approval step is refused in such a flow, unless its items are written out in the node and none of them is a record: an approval of each line, or of each of some other records, runs as an approval of its own, started apart from the document's. A flow that runs as the user does not write the record between two approval steps: such a flow is declared to run as system, or the write is moved. And no flow pauses between two approval steps, on a `wait` or a `screen`; the revise window of a returned request is not such a pause. Without these the platform refuses the request when an approval opens, and says what is missing ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)). Every approval step of the flow, and of the flows it calls, is judged at that moment. A refused opening reaches the person who submitted as a failed run: what the flow did before the step stays done, and where a change to the record started the flow, that save has landed. An object that closes on its approval field only on values of its own, `paid` or `void`, is asked none of this, and an abandoned approval still never puts such a value back. The mirror writes `pending` as each request opens. Approved, the document is closed again; on a flow with more than one approval step it is open again while the next step is pending. Rejected, it stays open unless the object closes on `rejected`. Returned, it is open for its submitter to revise. Abandoned, by its submitter's recall, by an administrator's override or by the release of a dead run, the document does not go back to a closed value: it reads `recalled` where the field declares that value, and otherwise the field's default, or empty where the default is a closed value or there is none. So an approval of a decided document that is then recalled costs the document its decision, also when it was started by mistake: it is approved again before it reads `approved` again. Listing `recalled` among a field's closed values does not close a recalled document, because the mirror writes no closed value for an abandoned approval. A `state_machine` rule on the approval field binds the mirror like any writer, and a move it refuses leaves a stale status on the document. An author who writes such a rule gives the mirror its moves: from one status to the next, and out of `pending` and `returned` for a request that is abandoned. An object that already runs approvals meets these conditions before, or together with, its closed values: from the moment the declaration is published, a new approval is refused until they hold. An approval flow none of whose steps names the field is asked nothing, and it does not open a closed document: a send-back in it gives the submitter nothing to revise, so its author gates its start on the state. An author who does not want a decided document sent for approval again gates the flow's start on the state, or closes on a business state the mirror does not write.
- **Code that calls the engine with no context is bound like a caller.** To write a closed document as the platform, it passes a system context.
- **An action button still works on a closed document.** An action's script body runs as system whoever clicks the button, so it changes a closed document as it would an open one. So does a flow or hook declared to run as system. The lock does not stop such a change and adds no notice or record of its own; the audit the app already keeps is where it is traced. `visible` and `disabled` on an action decide what the console shows, and the server does not read them (`content/docs/ui/actions.mdx`). An action that must not run on a closed document checks the state in its body. An action that updates declaratively, with no body, writes as the caller and is refused like an edit.
- **The way out is an action.** An app that wants users to reopen, void or refund ships the action and decides who may run it. The action needs a script body, or a flow that runs as system: a declarative update is the caller's own write, and it is refused.
- **A closed document keeps its owner.** Change Owner and the hand-over of a banned user's work are refused on it, record by record, and say so in their report ([D7](#d7--writes-that-are-not-a-callers-change)). An app that reassigns closed documents ships an action for it.
- **Line edits go before the closing write** when both travel in one save. A create has no such order: a caller cannot create a document that is already closed together with its lines, because each line would be added under a closed master. The caller creates it open, adds the lines and closes it last; a load of finished documents runs as system.
- **The lock read tells every reader of a document that it is closed.** A user who may read a record but not its state field is still told that it is closed, though not by which field or which value ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)). An app that hides a state field so that some readers do not learn the outcome, a portal user who must not see an internal decision, does not declare `closedStates` on that field.
- **A parent is not deleted while a closed document hangs below it.** The refusal names the parent and the kind of document in the way, not which document ([D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)). A user who cannot see it asks someone who can.

**What it costs**

- A write to a line reads its master's state fields, and a predicate write to a declaring object or its lines costs one existence probe. A write to an object that neither declares the key nor is a line of one reads nothing extra.
- There are two lock reads for as long as the approvals-family one is kept: the one read, with a seam between two packages behind it, and the approvals read that answers for approvals only. A client that asks only the second offers edits on closed documents, and the server refuses them.
- A reader whom field permissions keep from a state field learns one bit of it from the lock read: closed, or not ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)).
- A refused cascade names the kind of document in the way and not the document. Finding it is the user's work, or an administrator's ([D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)).
- An approval of a decided document that was then abandoned leaves the document open, even where nothing was edited meanwhile. It has to be approved again ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)).
- An approval on an object that closes on `approved` or `rejected` is refused when it opens until six things hold: `pending` is declared and open and `returned` is open, a `state_machine` rule on the field lets the closed outcome move to `pending`, the field carries no `readonlyWhen` and its options for a request's statuses no `visibleWhen`, every step that can act on the object names the one field, no flow that runs as the user writes the record between two steps, and no flow pauses there. An object that closes on `pending` or on `returned` runs no approval onto that field, and an approval the platform cannot read whole is refused ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)).
- An approval flow that runs as the user and writes the record between two steps has to be declared to run as system, or lose the write, before the object closes on its outcome.
- An approval that keeps one status field for each stage, or leaves a stage without one, has to bring its steps onto one field before the object closes on that field's outcome. A delay or a screen between two approval steps has to move out from between them.
- An approval of each line, or of each of some other records, under a `map` cannot run inside the approval of a document that closes on its approval's outcome. The check cannot read that every item is a record, so it refuses the `map` wherever it stands, and the per-item approval has to be started as an approval of its own. The same refusal meets a `map` body's step that names a field the parent's object closes on, also where the flow holds no other step on the parent's record.
- Three things the platform cannot read ahead could leave a document on an earlier step's outcome, closed, with its approval unfinished: a rule or a hook of the app that refuses a write of the mirror, a run that ends between two steps because a node fails or the run is cancelled, and a decision that is recorded while its run cannot be resumed. The key is not released before the card that deals with the three is built ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 8). A change to the object or to the flow after an approval opened is not judged again, and can leave the same ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not)).
- Three outcomes change in the showcase when it converts, and three refusals change their words ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 3): a caller can no longer edit a paid invoice's header, delete a paid invoice, or change any field of a line under one. No platform default changes; an app is affected only when it declares the key.

## Alternatives considered

**The key**

- **A predicate (`closedWhen`, a CEL expression).** More expressive, but "closed" would then fault, depend on related rows and need ADR-0137's fault semantics on every write and every read of the lock. The ruling says states; a list of states cannot fault and can be checked at publish.
- **A flag on each option of the state field.** Puts the lock in the field's option list, where a reader of the object does not look for it, and makes two-field declarations two separate edits.
- **A key on the `state_machine` rule, beside `initialStates`.** A validation rule has `events`, `severity` and `active`, and seeds skip it. A lock that can be switched off by deactivating a rule, or that a seed bypasses by construction, is not the declaration the ruling asks for.
- **`lockedStates`.** See [D1](#d1--the-key-closedstates).

**Who is bound** (▲ 1)

- **Elevated app automation may change only the state fields of a closed record.** Stronger: an action could not edit a closed document without reopening it first. It was not chosen because it splits "system" into two classes on this one path, turns "refund and stamp the refund" into three writes, and makes the showcase conversion change outcomes for system writes that the earlier ruling settled.
- **A caller may move the state along a declared `state_machine` transition.** Declarative, with no action to write. Not chosen because a transition says which moves are legal, not who may make them; every editor could reopen wherever a transition out of a closed state exists, including the ones declared for automation. It is also not the card's rule, which names a sanctioned action.
- **A request is not opened on a closed record.** The approvals plugin would refuse to open a request on a record in a declared closed state, so a new approval could not move a mirrored state on, and the author would have nothing to gate. Not chosen because sending a decided document for approval again, for a change order or a correction, is a path apps have, and refusing it changes the approvals plugin at its front door. [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not) keeps the path and tells the author where to gate it. The maintainer kept the path in the first two rulings of 2026-11-26. This reading has no design behind it and was not reviewed; taking it up later is a new decision.
- **An administrator grant that lifts the lock.** The pending lock refused this, and for the same reason: an override belongs in an audited action, not in an edit.
- **Bind system contexts too, as `readonlyWhen` does.** Leaves no way to move a closed document at all.

**What an approval writes on a closing field, and where it may open** ([D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not))

- **Leave the mirror as it is and leave it to the author**, who declares `recalled` on the field or gates the flow's start. Not chosen (ruled 2026-11-26): wherever an author missed the note, a closed value would come back over edits no approval has seen, and a lock that holds only where a note was read is not the platform's.
- **Read the rule as a rule on the put-back only**, and write `recalled` wherever the field declares it, also where the author lists `recalled` among the closed values. The ruling's own word is 恢复, restore, and `recalled` is the request's own status. Not chosen, on the seat's reading: the option the maintainer chose reads 「ADR 加一条：撤回永远不把状态写回关闭值」, the ADR adds a rule: a recall never writes the state back to a closed value, the ruling record words the rule the same way, and the rule exists so that a record with edits no approval has seen is not closed again, whatever value it would then show.
- **Write nothing, and leave the field at the value it holds.** The field would go on reading `pending` with no request open. That says an approval is in progress when none is, and an app that gates its flow's start on the field might never admit the document again.
- **Write `recalled` whether or not the field declares it.** The mirror is held to the values a field declares, on purpose (`packages/plugins/plugin-approvals/src/approval-service.ts#mirroredStatusValue` records why). A value the field does not declare is one the app's own gates and views have no word for.
- **Put the closed value back where the approval gave no window to edit**: a node that locks, and a request that was never returned. Kinder to an approval started by mistake. Not chosen because the mirror cannot show that nothing was edited: the pending lock passes the run that opened the request and every system context. It would also make one act end in two ways, by facts the user does not see. A rule a reader can state was preferred to a kinder one a reader cannot.
- **Write over whatever the field holds, as the mirror does today.** Simpler: the mirror would not read the field. Not chosen because it would open a record that another writer closed while the request was open. The mirror undoes what its own approval wrote and nothing else.
- **Draw the left-alone case otherwise**, as earlier revisions of this record did. Every closed value: that leaves a record on a closed value the approval itself wrote. Only a field that still holds its value at submit: a request does not always carry that value, and the test then has no answer. Every closed value but `pending` and `returned`, or every value no request of the same run had: an approval can take several steps and be spread over several runs, and one abandoned at a later step would leave the document on an earlier step's outcome, closed, reading a decision the approval did not finish. Today the same abandonment puts back the value held at submit. Every value no request of the approval had, whatever its record: under a `map` another item's request would then answer for this one.
- **Leave alone an earlier step's outcome where it is also the value held at submit**, which would keep the decision of a decided document. Not chosen: that the two are equal does not show the document stayed closed during the approval. A first step can send the document back, its submitter revises it in the revise window, and the step approves it when it is resubmitted; the kinder rule would then leave a closed `approved` over changes the later steps never saw. It would also bring back a reading of the value at submit, which a request does not always carry.
- **Put an empty value at submit back on every field**, as the mirror does today. Not chosen for a required field: where the field is not `readonly` the write is refused and the field stays where the approval left it, although it may declare a default the mirror can write; where it is `readonly` the write leaves a required field empty. On a field that is not required the empty value is still put back.
- **Accept a record left on an earlier step's outcome, with a note to authors**, as an earlier revision of this record did for the configurations in which the mirror has nothing to write. Not chosen (ruled 2026-11-26, ruling record [`7205949718`](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack/issues/36253#issuecomment-7205949718)): the record would read a decision, closed, that no finished approval stands behind, and a decision that holds only where a note was read is not the platform's. The second ruling of 2026-11-26 settled the same kind of question for the abandoned approval.
- **Refuse the configuration only where the object or the flow is published.** Not chosen as the rule: the two are published apart, and either can change after the other, so a publish check sees the pair only some of the time. It is kept as early notice.
- **Refuse only an approval of more than one step.** Not chosen: a single step fails too, on a decided document rejected into a status the field does not declare.
- **Judge each request as it opens, on its own step's field and its own record.** Not chosen: a later request refused would stop the run on the earlier step's outcome, which is the outcome the check exists to keep out. Judged at the first request only, and only on that request's own step, the check would pass over a later step that mirrors onto another field or acts on another record. So the whole approval is read at its first request.
- **Ask the conditions of every object that declares a closed value on the field**, as the first draft of this rule did. Not chosen: an object that closes on that field only on values of its own can be left on no unfinished decision, and the wider rule would refuse its approvals and ask its `state_machine` rule for a move out of a value it may mean to keep final.
- **Refuse a `readonlyWhen` on the mirror field only where it can hold on a closed value.** That is all the reason asks. Not chosen: a predicate can read any field of the record, the platform does not evaluate one ahead, and a rule that held for some predicates and not for others is not one an author can state. `readonly` does the work a mirror field needs.
- **Let the approval's own run pass the lock between two steps**, as the pending lock passes the run that opened its request. Not chosen: it would exempt a caller's context from this lock, which [D5](#d5--who-is-bound-every-caller-a-system-context-is-not) does for none, and a run cannot tell a node between two steps from the nodes after the last one, so every node after a decision would write a closed document as the decider.
- **Ask every approval of more than one step to run as system.** Simpler to check. Not chosen: it would elevate flows that write nothing between their steps.
- **Let the mirror answer for an earlier step's field as well as its own**, so that a later step with another field, or with none, still moves the field the earlier step closed. Not chosen: a request's statuses would then be written onto a field its own step does not name, which changes every write of the mirror and gives a mirror field a second meaning. One field for the whole approval asks nothing new of the mirror, and it is a rule an author can state.
- **Ask the one field only of the steps that come after a step the conditions concern.** Not chosen: a decided document that is sent for approval again opens on the outcome the last approval left. A first step with another field, or with none, lands no `pending` there, and the old decision stands, closed, through that step's rejection and through its revise window.
- **Place a step in a `map` body on the item's object alone**, where the node names one. Not chosen: whether an item is a record is known only as the body runs, and an item that is not one runs the body on the parent's record.
- **Take a `map` that names an object for its items as one whose items are all records, and let it stand where no step of the parent's record follows it.** The parent's steps are then finished when the items are asked, and its decision stands. Not chosen: the node decides item by item as the body runs, also where it names an object, so one collection can hold items of both kinds, and an item that is a record can fall between two openings of the body's step on the parent's record. Resting on the name would take a `map` that refuses an item of the other kind, which is a change to that node this record does not make.
- **Hold a run that waits on an approval of another record to the bound of item 8.** Not chosen, for the reason given for a `wait` and a `screen` below: the `map` is in the flow's definition, and what can be read ahead is refused when the approval opens.
- **Leave a `visibleWhen` on a status option to the card of item 8**, as one more refused write. Not chosen: it sits on the field's definition, where the check already reads `readonlyWhen`, and what can be read ahead is refused when the approval opens.
- **Refuse no pause, and hold a run parked between two steps to the bound of item 8.** Not chosen: a pause is in the flow's definition. And all the platform could do for a record during one is write an open value with no request open, which says an approval is waiting on a step nobody has been asked. The author moves the pause.
- **Let an object close on `pending` or `returned`**, as earlier revisions of this record did, and state the case in which an abandoned request then leaves the record closed with no request open. Not chosen: the pending lock already holds a record under approval and lets go in the revise window, and with both statuses open no failed write of the mirror leaves a lock behind.
- **Set the object's validation rules aside for the mirror's writes**, so that its last write always lands. Not chosen for every write of the mirror: an author's rule would then not hold against a platform write on any object, also one that declares no closed value, and that is a change to the mirror far wider than this record. The narrow form, only the write that takes a record off a closed value its own approval wrote, is not decided here: it is the first candidate of the card in [Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 8.
- **Hold a step's outcome back until the approval is over.** Not chosen: the mirror cannot know, when a step is decided, whether another follows.
- **Refuse the recall.** A submitter must be able to withdraw a request, and the sweep of a dead run cannot be refused at all: it exists to release the record.
- **Refuse, at publish, a closed mirror field that declares no `recalled` value.** The dependency runs across two artifacts published apart, an object and a flow, and the refusal would turn away a declaration the rule makes safe.

**Delete** (▲ 2)

- **Let the delete land, with its lines, as the pending lock does.** Consistent between the two locks, and the recycle bin makes it recoverable. Not chosen because the pending lock's rule exists to cancel a request, and because a document that can be deleted by anyone who may delete its object is not closed.
- **A per-object switch.** Item ① of the batch chose "no switch" for the lines; a switch here would be the first.
- **Judge each leg of a cascade behind that row's own gates.** The refusal would then be given only for a row the caller could have deleted, and it would disclose nothing the caller's own grants do not. Not chosen because the legs are deleted one at a time: on a store with no transactions, or where the cascade crosses two datasources, the rows already removed stay removed, and "refused whole" stops being true ([D6](#d6--a-closed-record-is-not-deleted-by-a-caller)).

**When it is judged** (▲ 3)

- **Apply item ③ to this lock: it holds if the record is closed before the write or would be closed after it.** Then a bound writer can neither leave a closed state nor enter one. A caller's write that sets `status` to `paid` is refused whole, with or without other fields, and so is a caller's create of a record already in a closed state. Only a system context closes a document: an action body, a flow or hook that runs as system, the approval mirror, a payment service.
  - *What it buys.* No document is closed for good by a slip in a form, and the closing-write-last ordering of [D4](#d4--what-a-closed-unit-refuses-and-when-it-is-judged) has nothing left to order.
  - *What it costs.* Every app that lets a user set the closing state in a form ships an action or automation for it before it declares the key. A load of finished documents runs as system. The refusal needs one more sentence, since "this document is closed" is false of a document that is still open.
  - *Why this record proposed the stored-row rule, and what the ruling took.* The card's rule says what sets the state ("approval, payment and closing only set the state") and not who may, and the stored-row rule leaves the road into a closed state where it is governed today, with the field's permissions and the `state_machine` rule. Item ③ costs a field lock one field, and the rest of the write lands; it would cost a record lock the whole closing write. And the two readings are not symmetric for an author. An app that wants closing reserved to an action can already withhold the state field from its users' edits through field permissions, which a system context does not meet. No app could lift a platform default that refused the closing write. The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-26 that item ③ governs `readonlyWhen` and not this lock.
  - *What the other choice changes in this record.* "A caller's own edit of the state field" in [D2](#d2--closed-is-read-off-the-stored-row-nothing-is-stored-for-it); in D4 the create row of the table, the bullet on creating a record in a closed state and the three bullets under ▲; the closing-write-last items in [Execution](#execution-after-acceptance) and [Consequences](#consequences), and one more sentence in [D8](#d8--the-refusal). Nothing else.
- **Judge against the state at the start of the unit of work**, so that one save may close the header first and write its lines after. Friendlier to a client that orders its writes header-first, but it needs per-unit memory in the engine and behaves differently on a store with no transactions. Ordering the closing write last gives the same result with no new mechanism.

**The read** (▲ 4)

- **Keep the read in the approvals plugin and have it ask the engine.** An app without approvals would then have closed states and no way to read them.
- **Two reads, one per producer.** Every client would have to call both and combine them, and each new door would have to remember the second.
- **Serve the approvals-family routes from the one read.** Kinder to a client that has not moved: it would grey Edit on a closed document. Not chosen because it changes what a published answer means. That read is documented as "held by a locking approval", and its `lockSource: 'record'` as "may still be deleted", which is false of a closed record ([D9](#d9--one-lock-read-owned-by-the-data-layer)). Its cost is that published contract's wording and every client that reads `lockSource` alone. Whether those routes retire or are redirected is a card of its own ([Execution](#execution-after-acceptance), item 6).
- **Say nothing of closed states to a reader who may not read the state field.** The read would answer such a reader for approvals only. It is the one reading that lets nothing of the field out through the read. Not chosen (ruled 2026-11-26) because of whom it protects. A reader who can write the record is offered Edit and Delete, meets the refusal, and is told there that the document is closed ([D8](#d8--the-refusal)). The reading protects only a reader who can neither write the record nor read the field, and for the others it brings back what the 2026-10-26 ruling removed: an edit offered and then refused.
- **Tell such a reader `locked` and withhold only the reason**, as an earlier revision of this record did. Not chosen: a lock with no reason promises more than it keeps, and it costs the client the means to tell in advance whether a delete or an upload will be refused.

**A line whose master the writer cannot read** ([D10](#d10--where-it-is-enforced-and-how-it-sits-beside-the-mechanisms-that-exist))

- **Refuse every write to a line that hangs under such a master, whatever the master's state.** This is how `readonlyWhen` treats a header the caller cannot read, and it tells the writer nothing about the master. Not chosen because it is a different lock. Declaring `closedStates` on a master would freeze the lines of its open documents for those writers, and the refusal would say a document is closed when it is not. The stored-state rule is the pending line lock's, and the one bit it gives goes to a writer the refusal is owed to.

**The status quo — each app writes its hooks.** That is the "B" of the earlier ruling, taken until a producer beyond the showcase was named. The 2026-11-22 ruling names them.
